<p>Most fences slow people down a little. A security fence is supposed to make getting in slow, loud, and obvious. That goal changes how we choose the material, the height, the top of the fence, and above all the gates.</p>
<p>Around Conroe we build security-minded fences for three kinds of owners: homeowners who want a backyard that is harder to get into, owners of lake houses and acreage that sit empty part of the week, and businesses with equipment, stock, or vehicles on the lot. The right type is different for each of them.</p>

<h2>Start with what you are protecting</h2>
<p>Before we talk materials, we ask what a break-in would actually look like. A contractor's yard off SH 105 worries about trailers and copper leaving at night. A retail lot along I-45 worries about after-hours loitering behind the building. A homeowner on a wooded lot backing to a trail in Harper's Preserve or Grand Central Park usually wants to stop foot traffic from the greenbelt side and keep the dog in.</p>
<p>Those answers decide how much of the budget goes to height, climb resistance, visibility, and gate control. A perimeter that is strong in one spot and weak in another is only as good as the weak spot, so we walk the whole line, including the back corner nobody looks at.</p>

<h2>Anti-climb mesh is not no-climb horse wire</h2>
<p>Acreage owners near Willis and Huntsville mix these up all the time. No-climb horse fence is woven wire with small openings so a horse can't put a hoof through it. It is a livestock fence on wood posts, and a person can still climb it. Welded anti-climb mesh is a rigid steel panel with very tight openings, built to keep people out.</p>
<p>If the real goal is protecting a shop full of tools on rural land, the better answer is usually a smaller security enclosure around the shop or equipment pad, with livestock fence handling the rest of the property. That costs far less than wrapping twenty acres in mesh. The livestock side is covered on our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage and ranch fencing</a> page.</p>

<h2>Height, tops, and what local rules allow</h2>
<p>Six to eight feet covers most commercial security perimeters, and the top matters as much as the height. Choices include spear-point pickets, angled arms with barbed strands, or a plain top rail. At a house, a 6 ft privacy or ornamental fence with its rails on the inside face already stops most casual climbers.</p>
<p>Local rules can take options off the table. The City of Cleveland's ordinance prohibits barbed wire and razor wire and caps rear and side fences at 8 ft. Panorama Village bans barbed wire and woven wire. Inside Conroe and Willis city limits, fences go through the city's permit process, and deed restrictions add their own limits. Texas law also restricts how far a property owners' association can block a homeowner's security fencing, though the association can still regulate the type. That is general information, not legal advice; read your own restrictions and ask an attorney if it turns into a dispute. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permits by jurisdiction</a> answer has more.</p>

<h2>Homes, lake houses, and wooded lots</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Subdivision backyards.</strong> A board-on-board or shadowbox fence with the rails facing in, plus a side-gate latch that takes a padlock, is the usual upgrade.</li>
<li><strong>East Lake Conroe and Lake Livingston houses.</strong> A weekend place is an easier target on a Tuesday. Open ornamental aluminum or steel keeps the water view while marking the line, and a locking drive gate does more than a taller back fence. On Lake Conroe, permanent structures on SJRA land below the 201 ft contour need an SJRA permit, so we stop the fence at your deeded line.</li>
<li><strong>Wooded lots.</strong> A tree right beside a fence is both a ladder and a hiding spot. Where we can, we set the line to leave a clear strip and keep limbs off the top.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Gates decide whether it works</h2>
<p>A perimeter is only as strong as its gate. Commercial sites usually do best with one controlled vehicle entrance, often a cantilever slide gate, plus a separate walk gate. Automatic gates at businesses fall under UL 325 Class II, and every automatic gate needs entrapment protection, which our <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-conroe-tx/">gate opener guide</a> explains. Plan for fire department access, keypad or card entry for staff, and a way to open the gate when the power is out.</p>
<p>Lights and cameras work with the fence, not instead of it. Where an owner wants to see into the lot from the road at night, we keep slats and solid panels out of those sightlines.</p>
