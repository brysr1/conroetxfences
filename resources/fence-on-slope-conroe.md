<p>A fence on a slope looks simple from the patio and gets complicated at the first post hole. Plenty of lots around here don't sit flat. Lake Conroe lots fall toward the water, wooded lots around Willis and Montgomery roll with the old creek drainages, and new subdivisions often grade backyards to drain toward a swale at the back line. Each of those changes how panels sit, how deep posts go, and where the gaps end up.</p>
<p>This guide covers the choices we walk through with Conroe-area owners before we stake a sloped line.</p>

<h2>Stepped or racked: two ways to build a fence on a slope</h2>
<p>Every sloped fence is built one of two ways, and many long runs use both.</p>

<h3>Stepped panels</h3>
<p>Each section stays level, and the fence drops down at every post like a staircase. The top line looks crisp and even, which many HOAs and homeowners like on a privacy fence. The tradeoff is a triangle-shaped gap under the low end of each section. The steeper the ground, the bigger that gap gets.</p>

<h3>Racked panels</h3>
<p>The rails run parallel to the ground while the pickets stay plumb, so the fence follows the hill. There are no stair-step gaps at the bottom, and on a long, even grade it looks natural. Wood built on site racks easily. Ornamental aluminum and steel panels can rack within the limits the manufacturer sets.</p>

<h2>Posts work harder on a grade</h2>
<p>Upland lots in Conroe, Willis, and Montgomery typically have sandy or fine sandy loam on top, often 18 to 30 inches or more of it, over a clay subsoil. Loose sand gives a post very little side grip to begin with. On a slope, the downhill side of each hole has even less soil bracing it, and stepped fences add another load because each section hangs a little taller on its low end.</p>
<p>That is why we don't treat sloped posts like flat-yard posts. Every post gets concrete, with 24 inches and 80 lb of concrete as the floor on wood fences. Corner and gate posts, and posts at the bottom of a steep step, get deeper, wider piers, belled at the base so they lock into firmer ground. If a hole fills with water from the clay below, we pump or bail it out before the pour. Our page on <a href="/resources/fence-posts-conroe-soil/">how we set posts in Conroe soil</a> goes into the full method.</p>

<h2>Closing the gaps under the fence</h2>
<p>On a stepped fence, those triangles at the bottom are where dogs get out and where neighbors' cats get in. There are a few clean ways to deal with them:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cut-to-grade rot board.</strong> A treated board along the bottom, trimmed to follow the ground, fills most of the gap and keeps pickets out of wet soil.</li>
<li><strong>Racking the worst stretches.</strong> If one section has a big drop, racking just that section often solves it.</li>
<li><strong>Wire along the bottom.</strong> For a determined digger, welded wire buried a few inches below grade stops tunneling.</li>
</ul>
<p>If a dog is the main reason for the fence, our <a href="/fence-styles/pet/">pet fence page</a> covers heights and dig guards in more detail.</p>

<h2>Gates on a sloped line</h2>
<p>A gate that swings toward the uphill side will drag on the ground partway through its swing. We look for the flattest spot on the line for the gate, hang it to swing downhill when we can, and leave enough clearance at the bottom for the full arc. A drive gate on a grade may need to be hung higher at one end or split into a double gate. <a href="/resources/how-wide-fence-gate/">Size the gate opening</a> for what has to pass through it before you settle on a spot.</p>

<h2>Keep runoff moving</h2>
<p>A slope means water is headed somewhere after a heavy rain. A solid fence built tight to the ground across that path acts like a small dam, piling up pine straw and sand against the boards and keeping the bottom wet. We leave the fence bottom open where water naturally runs and keep the line out of drainage swales and easements where we can.</p>
<p>Lakefront lots on Lake Conroe have one more line to respect. Shoreline land up to the 201-foot contour belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and a fence that slopes down toward the water can cross onto it without anyone noticing. We check the survey before we lay out the bottom end of a lake-lot fence.</p>
