<p>A gate is the one part of a fence that moves every day, and it hangs its whole weight off a single post. That is why it usually shows a problem first. Around Conroe the call tends to start the same way: the latch side has dropped an inch, the bottom scrapes the walk, and somebody in the house has started lifting the gate to close it.</p>
<p>The fix depends on what actually moved. Before you buy a kit or a new set of hinges, spend five minutes working out whether the trouble is the frame, the hardware, or the post.</p>

<h2>Read the gap before you touch anything</h2>
<p>Close the gate as far as it will go and study the gaps around it.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Wide at the top of the latch side, tight at the bottom.</strong> The frame has racked out of square, or the hinges have pulled loose from the wood.</li>
<li><strong>The gate is square but sits low, and the hinge post tips toward the opening.</strong> The post has moved.</li>
<li><strong>The latch misses by a little, but the gate swings freely.</strong> Something shifted slightly, often new posts settling or ordinary seasonal movement.</li>
</ul>
<p>Then hold a level against the hinge post. If it is out of plumb, no amount of hinge work will hold for long.</p>

<h2>When the frame or hardware is the problem</h2>
<p>Wood gates sag because the boards and rails want to fold into a parallelogram under their own weight. A diagonal brace running from the bottom hinge corner up to the top latch corner fights that. If a gate was built without one, or with the brace running the wrong direction, adding it properly often cures the sag.</p>
<p>Other fixes that work:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Retighten or move the hinges.</strong> Screws strip out of soft or damp wood. Shifting a hinge slightly onto fresh wood, or switching to longer exterior screws, restores the grip.</li>
<li><strong>Add an anti-sag kit.</strong> A cable and turnbuckle run corner to corner lets you crank the latch side back up. It suits a lightly built gate, not one hanging on a leaning post.</li>
<li><strong>Upgrade the hinges.</strong> Hinges sized for a small garden gate bend under a tall board-on-board leaf. Heavy strap hinges rated for the weight spread the load along the frame.</li>
<li><strong>Rebuild on a steel frame.</strong> For a gate that gets daily use, a welded steel frame with wood pickets on the face stays square far longer than an all-wood frame.</li>
</ul>
<p>Shade plays a part too. On wooded lots, gates under the pines stay damp, and a frame that never dries out loosens at every joint. Keeping the gate stained, and keeping pine straw and mulch away from its bottom edge, helps it hold.</p>

<h2>If the hinge post has moved</h2>
<p>A leaning hinge post means the gate keeps dropping no matter what you bolt to it. A gate post takes a sideways pull every time the gate swings open. In the sandy ground common around Conroe, with slow-draining clay below, that pull can work a shallow or undersized post loose, especially after a long wet stretch.</p>
<p>The real repair is a new hinge post, set deeper than the line posts and in more concrete. On a gate that has failed more than once, or a wide and heavy one, we often put a steel post on the hinge side. Diagnosing the post itself works the same way as anywhere else on the fence, and our <a href="/resources/fix-leaning-fence-post-conroe/">leaning post guide</a> covers it.</p>

<h2>Wide gates for boats, trailers, and mowers</h2>
<p>In lake neighborhoods like <a href="/neighborhoods/seven-coves/">Seven Coves</a> and Point Aquarius, the side gate is often how a boat or trailer gets into the backyard. On acreage near Huntsville and Lake Livingston, a gate may need to pass a tractor. Those openings run much wider than a walk gate, and a single wide wood leaf is heavy enough to sag quickly.</p>
<p>Two leaves split the weight across two posts. A double gate needs a drop rod on the inactive leaf so it stays put while the other swings, plus a sleeve or plate in the ground so the rod has something solid to drop into. A steel frame under the wood face is the most reliable way to keep a wide gate square for years.</p>

<h2>Building the next gate so it stays square</h2>
<p>If you are replacing a gate, these details matter more than the picket style:</p>
<ol>
<li>Size the opening for the biggest thing that will go through it.</li>
<li>Give the gate posts more depth and more concrete than the line posts get.</li>
<li>Use a braced wood frame or a welded steel frame.</li>
<li>Pick hinges rated for the gate&rsquo;s real weight.</li>
<li>Leave enough clearance underneath for the slope of the walk and the pine straw that will pile up.</li>
<li>Choose a latch with some room to adjust as the posts settle.</li>
</ol>
