<p>A commercial fence job involves more people than a backyard. There is usually an owner, a property manager or general contractor, a city or county reviewer, sometimes a landlord, and the people who still need to drive in and out every day while we work. Getting the paperwork and the schedule right matters as much as setting the posts.</p>
<p>This guide covers how we plan commercial work around Conroe, from highway frontage on I-45 and Loop 336 to yards off SH 105 and FM 1314, plus sites in Willis, Huntsville, and Cleveland.</p>

<h2>Know which rules apply to the parcel</h2>
<p>Commercial parcels around Conroe fall under different authorities, sometimes on the same stretch of road.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>City of Conroe.</strong> Fences are regulated under Chapter 14, Article XI of the Code of Ordinances, and permits go through Building Inspections and Permits. Check whether your approved site plan already shows a fence or screening requirement.</li>
<li><strong>City of Willis.</strong> The fee schedule lists $75 for wood or chain link fences and $175 for a masonry fence wall, and the fee doubles if work starts before the permit is issued.</li>
<li><strong>City of Cleveland.</strong> Residential and commercial fences both need a permit. Rear and side fences can be up to 8 ft and front fences up to 6 ft, with open-view rules above 4 ft, and barbed wire, razor wire, and plywood are prohibited.</li>
<li><strong>Huntsville.</strong> Fence rules sit in the city's development code, and we confirm them with the city for each parcel.</li>
<li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> County permits focus on enclosed structures and floodplain development, so a fence inside a mapped floodplain may need county review.</li>
</ul>
<p>We check the parcel before the quote goes out, and our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permits by jurisdiction</a> page has more detail. Treat this as general information, not legal advice.</p>

<h2>Match the fence to the property</h2>
<h3>Retail and office frontage</h3>
<p>On a lot facing I-45 or Loop 336, the fence is part of how the business looks from the road. Ornamental steel or aluminum along the front keeps signs and storefronts visible, while heavier chain link or mesh runs the sides and back where customers don't look.</p>
<h3>Contractor yards, storage, and equipment lots</h3>
<p>Yards off SH 105, FM 1314, and other working corridors usually need 6 to 8 ft commercial chain link or welded mesh, an anti-climb top where it is allowed, and a slide gate wide enough for trailers. Privacy slats hide equipment but load the posts with wind. Our <a href="/resources/security-fence-types/">security fence types</a> guide compares the options.</p>
<h3>HOA amenity centers and apartment pools</h3>
<p>Pools, clubhouses, and amenity lakes in master-planned communities need fencing that fits the community's look and meets code. Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 757 applies to pools run by property owners' associations and multiunit rental complexes. It calls for a barrier at least 48 inches high and gates that self-close, self-latch, open outward, and lock, and it bars chain link on enclosures built after 1994. Latch height and opening sizes have their own rules. See <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosures</a>.</p>
<h3>Dumpster enclosures</h3>
<p>Restaurants, retail centers, churches, and amenity centers often need a screened enclosure with gates the hauler's truck can use without hitting a post. Gate and corner posts take repeated contact, so they get bigger footings. See <a href="/dumpster-enclosures/">dumpster enclosures</a>.</p>

<h2>Gates and access control</h2>
<p>Automatic gates at commercial and public sites fall under UL 325 Class II. That brings the same monitored entrapment protection a home gate needs, built for heavier traffic. Decide how many vehicle entrances you really need, since one controlled entrance is easier to secure than three. Plan a separate pedestrian gate, fire department and emergency access, and how deliveries get in after hours.</p>
<p>If an access-control company is installing card readers or a call box, we plan pads, conduit paths, and post locations with them, so nothing has to be cut out later.</p>

<h2>Scheduling around a business that stays open</h2>
<p>Most commercial clients can't close the lot while we work. We phase the job so customers can still park, trucks can still get to the loading area, and the lot is secure again before the crew leaves each evening. On a replacement, the old fence comes out in sections so the perimeter isn't left open overnight.</p>
<p>Texas 811 locates come first, and a commercial lot usually hides far more underground than a house lot. Irrigation, parking-lot lighting, sign power, and private water lines aren't marked by 811, so ask your facilities team for site drawings if they exist.</p>
