<p>The chain link vs. wood fence question usually comes down to one thing: what you want the fence to do. If it only needs to keep a dog in or mark a line, chain link does that for less money and almost no upkeep. If you want privacy, a yard that feels like an outdoor room, or something that looks finished from the patio, wood wins. Around Conroe, Willis, and Montgomery we build plenty of both, and many lots end up with some of each.</p>

<h2>Cost: chain link vs. wood fence per foot</h2>
<p>On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, chain link runs roughly $18 to $40 per installed foot, treated pine $25 to $35, and cedar $30 to $47. The low end of chain link is bare galvanized wire at a standard height. Black vinyl-coated mesh, taller fabric, and heavier wire push it up toward wood prices.</p>
<p>The gap matters most on long runs. On a half-acre or acre lot in the county, where a fence line can run several hundred feet, saving even a few dollars a foot adds up fast. On a typical subdivision backyard the gap is smaller, so privacy often decides it.</p>

<h2>Privacy and looks</h2>
<p>Wood blocks the view. That is why most backyards in newer Conroe subdivisions are pine or cedar, especially where a neighbor's back windows look down into the yard.</p>
<p>Chain link hides nothing on its own. Bright galvanized wire looks like a work fence, which is fine on a back line or around a garden. Black coated mesh with black posts is a different story. Against a treeline of pines, it nearly disappears from the porch, so it suits lots where you want to keep a view of woods, a pond, or a pasture. Privacy slats help when you need some screening on a budget, though they never look quite like a solid fence.</p>

<h2>Lifespan and upkeep in a humid climate</h2>
<p>Water and sun wear wood out. Pine commonly lasts about 8 to 12 years here and cedar about 12 to 18, with shaded, wet lots at the short end. Wood also needs a wash and a fresh coat of stain every few years to stay sound and look good.</p>
<p>Chain link has nothing to seal. Galvanizing slows rust in our humid air, and a vinyl coating adds another layer. Rust shows first at cut ends and scratched fittings. The real upkeep is pulling vines off the mesh before they stretch it and adjusting a gate now and then. Under mature pines, a falling limb can kink a top rail, which is a quick fix.</p>

<h2>Wind, storms, and sandy ground</h2>
<p>Wind passes straight through chain link, so a strong storm puts little load on the posts. A 6-foot wood privacy fence catches the full force of the wind, and that load lands on the posts at ground level. Add slats to chain link and it starts acting like a solid fence again.</p>
<p>That matters on our ground. Upland lots in Conroe, Willis, and Montgomery usually have 18 to 30 inches or more of loose sandy loam over a clay and plinthite subsoil, and loose sand doesn't grip a post sideways. On a wood fence, gate and corner posts lean first. On chain link, the corner, end, and gate posts carry the tension of the whole run. Either way, those posts get deeper, wider piers belled at the base. Every post goes in concrete, never gravel. On wood fences, 24 inches and 80 lb of concrete per post is the floor, and gates and corners get more. Any water in the hole gets pumped or bailed out before the pour. Our guide to <a href="/resources/fence-posts-conroe-soil/">setting fence posts in Conroe soil</a> explains why.</p>

<h2>HOA rules and where each one fits</h2>
<p>Many master-planned communities around Conroe limit chain link, especially where it faces a street, and some allow it only in coated black or not at all. Rural tracts and county-road lots usually have fewer limits. Rules change and vary by community, so we check the current guidelines for your address before quoting.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Chain link fits</strong> dog runs, back and side lines on acreage, garden and chicken enclosures, and shop or equipment yards.</li>
<li><strong>Wood fits</strong> subdivision backyards, patios and pool areas you want screened, and anywhere the HOA expects a privacy fence.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The practical takeaway</h2>
<p>If privacy or looks matter, choose wood. If the fence is mostly about containment on a long line, chain link saves real money and time. On a lot of Conroe-area properties, the best answer is both: a wood privacy fence around the backyard and patio, and black coated chain link on the back acreage or along the woods.</p>
