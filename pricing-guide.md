    <div class="lc-head">
      <p class="lc-kicker">Around Conroe</p>
      <h2>Local factors that change the price</h2>
      <p class="lc-lede">Two fences with the same footage can end up with very different prices. Around Conroe and the lake, these are the factors that most often explain the difference.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="lc-grid lc-grid-3">
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>Wooded lots and clearing</h3><p>Many lots here back to pine and hardwood woods or greenbelt. Brush along the line may need clearing, trees may need to be worked around, and roots slow down hole digging. We note all of this at the site visit and include any clearing in the quote.</p></div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>Lake-lot rules</h3><p>On Lake Conroe, SJRA land comes up to the 201-foot contour, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit. Lakefront and canal lots may also have bulkheads, slopes down to the water, and HOA view guidelines. Each of these can affect the layout, the material, and how long the job takes.</p></div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>Long acreage runs</h3><p>On acreage north and east of town, the cost per foot often drops while the total climbs. Brace assemblies, wide drive gates, creek crossings, and getting equipment onto the land all show up in the quote.</p></div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>HOA-required styles or stain</h3><p>If your community calls for a specific style, height, cap, or stain color, the material list follows those rules. We confirm the current guidelines for your lot, then price the fence the committee will approve.</p></div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>Removing an old builder fence</h3><p>Many subdivisions came with a basic builder fence, and plenty of those now lean. Pulling it out, breaking up old post footings, and hauling it away takes labor. The quote shows that cost separately.</p></div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body"><h3>Grade, soil, and water</h3><p>Sloped yards need stepped or racked panels. In sandy soil or low, wet ground, posts need deeper or wider holes. Lots near the West Fork or other low ground call for a layout that lets water through.</p></div></div>
    </div>

    <div>
      <p class="lc-kicker">Built in, not added on</p>
      <h2>What goes into our wood fences</h2>
      <p>Every cedar or pressure-treated pine fence we build includes the following:</p>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
        <li><strong>Three rails, not two.</strong> The extra rail stiffens the fence and helps keep pickets from warping.</li>
        <li><strong>A rot board at the bottom.</strong> It is 6&Prime; tall as standard, and you can choose a taller one.</li>
        <li><strong>Posts sunk a minimum of 24&Prime;</strong>, each one set with 80&nbsp;lb of concrete. Loose sand or wet ground gets deeper or wider holes.</li>
        <li><strong>Simpson brackets</strong> connect rails to posts, so the joints are not just toe-nailed.</li>
        <li><strong>Hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank nails</strong> hold their grip and resist rust.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>These details explain much of where our prices start. On a cheap fence, rails often pull loose from posts first, and bare nails leave rust streaks down the boards. Paying for them at the start costs less than paying for repairs later.</p>
    </div>
    <div>
      <p class="lc-kicker">Comparing quotes</p>
      <h2>When one bid is far lower</h2>
      <p>Lumber, steel, and concrete cost about the same no matter who buys them. A bid that is far below the others usually saves money on something: fewer rails, shallower posts, less concrete, thinner pickets or wire, or a company that will be hard to reach when a gate needs adjusting.</p>
      <p>Put these to every contractor you talk to:</p>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
        <li>What depth are posts set at, and how many pounds of concrete go into each one?</li>
        <li>How are the rails attached to the posts?</li>
        <li>Who handles HOA paperwork and 811 locates?</li>
        <li>What does the written warranty cover?</li>
      </ul>
      <p>You can find our answers on the <a href="{{ '/process/' | relative_url }}">process</a> and <a href="{{ '/warranty/' | relative_url }}">warranty</a> pages.</p>
