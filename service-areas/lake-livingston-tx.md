      <h2>A big lake with a lot of shoreline to fence</h2>
      <p>Lake Livingston covers about 83,000 acres, with more than 450 miles of shoreline across Polk, San Jacinto, Walker, and Trinity counties. The dam went in between 1966 and 1969, about 7 miles southwest of the city of Livingston, and the Trinity River Authority runs the lake under a contract with the City of Houston.</p>
      <p>There is no single lake town. Livingston, the Polk County seat, counted 5,640 residents at the 2020 census and sits on US 59. Coldspring is on the southwest side of the lake, and Trinity is in Trinity County. Around them are lake communities of very different ages and sizes, several dating to the 1970s, reached by SH 150, FM 980, FM 356, FM 3277, and SH 146.</p>
      <p>From our Conroe office, Livingston is about 52 miles northeast by way of SH 105 and US 59. Coldspring is about 40 miles. Lots here run from small wooded homesites in older resort communities to bulkheaded waterfront acreage of an acre or more. If yours is a weekend place, it pays to pick a fence you don't have to think about between visits.</p>

      <h2>Four counties, many POAs, and one river authority</h2>
      <p>Whether your lot is inside a city or out in the county matters, and the lake touches four counties. On top of that, most of the communities we serve here have a POA, deed restrictions, or an architectural committee, and covenants can differ from one section or subdivision to the next.</p>
      <p>Waterfront adds one more layer. The Trinity River Authority manages the lake, and work along the shoreline may involve TRA rules. We confirm what applies to your shoreline before we build near it.</p>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
