      <h2>Huntsville from a fence builder's view</h2>
      <p>Huntsville is the seat of Walker County, about 31 miles north of Conroe on I-45. It counted 45,822 people at the 2020 census, and recent estimates put it a little over 49,000. Sam Houston State University and the Texas Department of Criminal Justice headquarters shape the town, and the Sam Houston National Forest fills much of the ground between here and Conroe.</p>
      <p>I-45 is the spine. SH 75 runs into town as Sam Houston Ave. (exit 112 is the turn for <a href="/neighborhoods/elkins-lake/">Elkins Lake</a> on the south side), SH 30 passes <a href="/neighborhoods/the-reserve-at-huntsville/">The Reserve at Huntsville</a> near the university, and FM 1374 leads south to <a href="/neighborhoods/texas-grand-ranch/">Texas Grand Ranch</a>.</p>
      <p>Much of the ground is wooded and rolling. In Elkins Lake, elevation runs from about 352 to 505 feet across the community, so fence lines climb, step down, and cross ravines. Out on the acreage, lots of two to five acres have seasonal creeks and timber, and fences there have more to do with horses and drive gates than with neighbors.</p>

    <h2>City limits, county land, and POAs</h2>
    <p>Huntsville properties fall into two broad camps. Some, like Elkins Lake, are inside city limits. Others, including most acreage south of town, are in unincorporated Walker County. A deed-restricted subdivision or POA can add its own fence review on top of either one.</p>
    <p>We don't guess at heights, setbacks, or permit steps here. Before we quote, we confirm with the City of Huntsville or Walker County what applies to your address, get any association guidelines, and request Texas 811 locates before anyone digs.</p>
