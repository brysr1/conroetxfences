      <p class="lc-kicker">Rules &amp; permits</p>
      <h2>City, county, or HOA: who has a say</h2>
      <p>Around Conroe, the address on your mail doesn't tell you which rules apply. Three layers can come into play, and a single lot can have more than one.</p>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
        <li><strong>Inside city limits.</strong> The City of Conroe's Building Inspections and Permits department handles fence questions for property within the city. We confirm with the department what a given project needs.</li>
        <li><strong>Unincorporated county.</strong> Many subdivisions with Conroe addresses are outside the city, often served by a MUD. City fence rules don't reach them, but deed restrictions still do.</li>
        <li><strong>HOA or POA.</strong> Almost every newer community here has an association or architectural committee that reviews fences. We submit the details and wait for approval.</li>
        <li><strong>Other cities and the lake.</strong> <a href="/neighborhoods/panorama-village/">Panorama Village</a> is its own city with its own ordinances. On Lake Conroe lots, land up to the 201-foot contour belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit.</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="lc-note">We don't quote height or permit rules from memory. We check the current requirements for your address before we price the job, and Texas 811 marks buried lines before anyone digs.</p>
