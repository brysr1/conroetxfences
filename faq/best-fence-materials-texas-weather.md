<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> For weather alone, aluminum and vinyl need the least from you here, and powder-coated steel is the strongest when it is kept touched up. Wood is still the most popular backyard fence around Conroe and does fine when it is built with a rot board, sound posts, and regular sealing. The way the posts are set matters more than the material choice.</p>
</div>

<h2>What a fence is up against here</h2>
<p>NOAA's 1991&ndash;2020 normals for the <a href="https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/#dataset=normals-monthly&amp;timeframe=30&amp;station=USC00411956" rel="noopener">Conroe weather station</a> put the average high at 94.9&deg;F in July and 95.8&deg;F in August, with about 51 inches of rain a year. May, June, and October are the wettest months, at more than 5 inches each. Atlantic hurricane season runs <a href="https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/" rel="noopener">June 1 through November 30</a>, and the ground under most yards is loamy sand over slower-draining clay. So a fence here bakes in summer, sits wet at the base for days in spring and fall, and has to take the occasional tropical gust.</p>

<h2>Where your location changes the answer</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Wooded lots in Conroe and Willis.</strong> Shade and pine straw keep the base of a fence wet in neighborhoods like Harper's Preserve and Graystone Hills, so wood needs more care here than on an open lot. Many owners pair wood pickets with galvanized steel posts so the part that sits in the ground cannot rot.</li>
  <li><strong>Lake Conroe shoreline lots.</strong> Water Crest, Lake Conroe Hills, Seven Coves, and Point Aquarius all get damp air and open-water wind. Aluminum is our usual pick close to the water.</li>
  <li><strong>Low lots along the West Fork.</strong> River Plantation has flooded more than once, including 1994, 2015, and 2016. Where water can move across a yard, open metal or chain link lets it through instead of pushing a solid panel over. See <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">fencing in flood zones and easements</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Cleveland and Lake Livingston.</strong> Texas A&amp;M AgriLife lists Liberty and Polk among the counties where the <a href="https://urbanentomology.tamu.edu/urban-pests/termites/formosan/" rel="noopener">Formosan subterranean termite</a> has been detected. Wood still works there, but a rot board, steel posts, or a non-wood material cuts down the wood that touches the ground.</li>
  <li><strong>Huntsville and the national forest edge.</strong> Elkins Lake runs from about 352 to 505 feet in elevation, and much of the land around Huntsville, including the Texas Grand Ranch acreage, is wooded and rolling. That means more falling limbs and stepped panels, so materials that repair section by section save money over time.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The build details that matter in any material</h2>
<p>Weather finds the weak point, and on most fences that is the post. Our wood-fence posts sit a minimum of 24 inches down in 80 pounds of concrete apiece, with bigger holes in loose sand or wet ground. Gate and corner posts, and posts on taller fences, go deeper still. Three rails per section, a 6-inch rot board, Simpson brackets, and hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank nails keep the frame tight through wet and dry spells. For more on the ground itself, see <a href="/resources/fence-posts-conroe-soil/">how we set posts in Conroe soil</a>. Material specifics are on the <a href="/materials/">materials page</a>, and our <a href="/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/">ornamental steel and aluminum</a> page covers the metal options near water.</p>
