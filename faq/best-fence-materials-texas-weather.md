<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> For weather alone, aluminum and vinyl need the least from you here, and powder-coated steel is the strongest when it is kept touched up. Wood is still the most popular backyard fence around Conroe and does fine when it is built with a rot board, sound posts, and regular sealing. The way the posts are set matters more than the material choice.</p>
</div>

<h2>How each material handles our conditions</h2>
<p>Conroe summers bring highs in the mid-90s, about 51 inches of rain a year, and a hurricane season that runs June 1 through November 30. Most of the ground is loamy sand over slower-draining clay. This table sums up what we see from each material on the job.</p>

<h2>Where your location changes the answer</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Wooded lots in Conroe and Willis.</strong> Shade and pine straw keep the base of a fence wet, so wood needs more care here than on an open lot. Many owners pair wood pickets with galvanized steel posts so the part that sits in the ground cannot rot.</li>
  <li><strong>Lake Conroe shoreline lots.</strong> Water Crest, Lake Conroe Hills, Seven Coves, and Point Aquarius all get damp air and open-water wind. Aluminum is our usual pick close to the water.</li>
  <li><strong>Cleveland and Lake Livingston.</strong> Texas A&amp;M lists Formosan subterranean termites as established in Liberty and Polk counties. Wood still works there, but a rot board, steel posts, or a non-wood material cuts down the wood that touches the ground.</li>
  <li><strong>Huntsville and the national forest edge.</strong> Hilly, wooded lots mean more falling limbs and stepped panels. Materials that repair section by section save money over time.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The build details that matter in any material</h2>
<p>Weather finds the weak point, and on most fences that is the post. Our wood-fence posts sit a minimum of 24 inches down in 80 pounds of concrete apiece, with bigger holes in loose sand or wet ground. Gate and corner posts go deeper still. Hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank nails and metal rail brackets keep the frame tight through wet and dry spells. For more on the ground itself, see <a href="/resources/fence-posts-conroe-soil/">how we set posts in Conroe soil</a>. Material specifics are on the <a href="/materials/">materials page</a>, and our <a href="/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/">ornamental steel and aluminum</a> page covers the metal options near water.</p>
