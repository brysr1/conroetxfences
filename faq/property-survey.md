<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> You don't always need a new one. If you have the survey from your closing and the corner pins can be found, that's usually enough to set a fence on a subdivision lot. A fresh survey earns its cost when the line is in doubt: a neighbor disagrees, the land is acreage, an old fence wanders, or the lot fronts water where the deeded line may stop short of the shore.</p>
</div>

<h2>Start with the paperwork from your closing</h2>
<p>Most owners around Conroe already have a survey in the closing packet from the title company. It shows the lot lines, easements, the house footprint, and the iron rods set at the corners. Bring it to the estimate, or text us a photo ahead of time, and we'll compare it with what's in the ground.</p>
<p>Finding those rods is the part that varies by neighborhood. On a newer lot along the SH 242 corridor, a rod can get knocked loose by a landscaping crew or covered by fresh sod. On older wooded streets in Graystone Hills, Harper's Preserve, or Lake Chateau Woods, they sit under years of pine straw and roots. A metal detector finds most of them. If every corner turns up and they match the drawing, a new survey rarely changes anything.</p>

<h2>When we'd tell you to order one</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A neighbor questions the line.</strong> A stamped survey settles it before posts go in, which costs less than moving a fence later. For splitting the bill, see <a href="/faq/property-line-fence-cost/">who pays for a property line fence</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Acreage north and east of town.</strong> Around Huntsville, Willis, and Cleveland, old wire fences often follow a tree line or dodge a wet draw instead of the deed. Our <a href="/acreage-ranch-fencing/">acreage fencing</a> work starts from a line you're sure of.</li>
  <li><strong>Two or more pins are missing.</strong> A surveyor can reset them, which is often less work than a full new survey.</li>
  <li><strong>Your city asks for it.</strong> Cleveland's <a href="https://tx-cleveland.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/3808" rel="noopener">fence permit application</a> requires a site plan showing property lines and says the city may require a survey. Panorama Village asks for a plot plan with lot lines and setbacks; our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permit guide</a> has the details.</li>
  <li><strong>Easements cross the yard.</strong> Drainage and utility easements show on the survey and plat, and some limit fences. See <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">fencing in flood zones and easements</a>.</li>
</ul>
<p>On wooded tracts, a survey also tells you where you can clear. Texas A&amp;M AgriLife's fence-law guide, <a href="https://agrilife.org/texasaglaw/files/2022/07/Second-Edition-Five-Strands.pdf" rel="noopener"><em>Five Strands</em></a>, calls an accurate survey "paramount" for boundary fences. It also warns that clearing brush on the neighbor's side without permission can be trespass, so on a lot like those in Big Thicket Lake Estates or Deer Forest, the line decides where the saw stops.</p>

<h2>Lake Conroe lots and the 201-foot line</h2>
<p>Shoreline lots are where a survey matters most. SJRA's <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf" rel="noopener">Lake Conroe Rules and Regulations</a> define Authority land as the reservoir and the land next to it up to the 201-foot contour. Under those rules, a structure permanently placed on that land without an SJRA permit is a violation. SJRA can order it removed at the owner's expense, and a violation is a Class C misdemeanor with fines of $25 to $500 per day. Your deeded line may stop at that contour, which can sit well back from where the lawn meets the water in Point Aquarius, Seven Coves, or Water Crest. A survey that shows the 201 line, or a surveyor who stakes it, keeps every post on your side.</p>

<h2>Lake Livingston lots</h2>
<p>Lake Livingston runs on its own numbers. The Trinity River Authority manages it, and TRA lists normal pool at <a href="https://trinityra.org/doing_business_with_us/property_owner_permits_and_licenses_at_lake_livingston.php" rel="noopener">131 feet above sea level</a>, with permits for shoreline structures handled through its property owner permits page. For a waterfront fence in Cape Royale, Waterwood, or Westwood Shores, we check with TRA before staking anything.</p>

<h2>Choosing a surveyor and weighing the cost</h2>
<p>Boundary surveys in Texas are done by Registered Professional Land Surveyors, and you can check one on the state board's <a href="https://pels.texas.gov/roster/rplssearch.html" rel="noopener">RPLS roster</a>. Fees depend on lot size, tree cover, and how much research the deed needs, so get a quote from a local surveyor. Weigh it against the alternative: a fence a foot onto the neighbor's lot can mean a removal demand or a sale that stalls at title review. On a small lot with known pins, you can usually skip it. On five wooded acres or a lakefront lot, order one.</p>
