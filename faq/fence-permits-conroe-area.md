<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> It depends on which government your lot actually sits in, and the mailing address won't tell you. Inside Conroe, Willis, Panorama Village, Huntsville, or Cleveland, the city's fence rules apply. Out in unincorporated Montgomery County, a fence outside the floodplain generally doesn't need a county permit, though deed restrictions, an HOA, and SJRA or TRA rules on lake lots still can.</p>
</div>

<h2>Step one: find your jurisdiction</h2>
<p>Conroe ZIP codes reach far past the city limits. Many newer subdivisions and much of the lake shore sit in unincorporated Montgomery County. Panorama Village is a separate city even though its mail says Conroe, and several east-shore lake communities with Willis addresses appear to sit outside Willis city limits. Your appraisal district record or a call to the city settles it, and we confirm it before the quote is final.</p>

<h2>The layers on top of a permit</h2>
<p>A city permit and an HOA approval are separate reviews. In Grand Central Park, Artavia, Mavera, or Harper's Preserve, the architectural committee looks at the fence whatever the city requires. On the Lake Conroe shore, SJRA rules govern land below the 201-foot contour. On Lake Livingston, shoreline work goes through the Trinity River Authority, and the lot itself may be in Polk, San Jacinto, Walker, or Trinity County. See <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">flood zones and easements</a> for those limits.</p>
<p>Pools add one more set. Cleveland has adopted the 2015 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code, and Panorama Village requires pool fences under its adopted codes. Our <a href="/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/">pool enclosure</a> page covers the build side.</p>
