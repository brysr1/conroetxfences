<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> For most dogs around Conroe, a 6-foot solid wood or vinyl fence with a dig barrier along the base and self-latching gates does the job. Small dogs need tight spacing at the bottom, jumpers need height with nothing to climb on, and diggers need protection below grade. On lots where the HOA requires an open fence, a mesh liner or close picket spacing fills the gaps.</p>
</div>

<h2>Match the fence to how your dog escapes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Squeezers.</strong> Small breeds and puppies fit through 3- or 4-inch gaps. Board-on-board wood, solid vinyl, or ornamental panels with extra pickets at the bottom close those gaps.</li>
  <li><strong>Jumpers and climbers.</strong> Athletic dogs that clear 4 feet need 6. Keep rails, planters, and AC pads off the inside of the fence line so there is no step.</li>
  <li><strong>Diggers.</strong> Covered in the next section, since our soil makes this the most common problem.</li>
  <li><strong>Barkers.</strong> A solid fence blocks the view of passing dogs, joggers, and wildlife in the greenbelt, which calms many reactive dogs.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Sandy ground and diggers</h2>
<p>The most common soil in Montgomery County is Conroe loamy fine sand, and it digs fast. A determined dog can tunnel under a fence in an afternoon. Heavy rain makes it worse: water running along the fence line washes sand out from under the bottom board, and that small gap is where many escapes start. Pine roots can lift the ground and open gaps too.</p>
<p>For diggers, we use a buried wire apron or a barrier set below grade along the base, and we close gaps under the bottom board when we build. After a heavy rain, walk the line and fill any new washouts. Utility lines get marked through Texas 811 before anything goes in the ground.</p>

<h2>When the HOA wants an open fence</h2>
<p>Lots that back to lakes, ponds, or greenbelts in communities like Grand Central Park, Harper's Preserve, and Artavia often have rear-fence rules that favor open styles. Open ornamental fencing keeps the view but can let a small dog through. The fix is usually a mesh liner or puppy pickets near the ground, if your guidelines allow it.</p>
<p>Panorama Village is one place where the rule is written down. Its fence permit packet says golf-course lots must use a 48-inch black wrought iron or aluminum fence, and it allows black mesh on the bottom 12 inches. For a small dog, that mesh matters. For a larger dog that can clear 48 inches, a fenced patio area closer to the house may work better. We check the current rules for your address before we design.</p>

<h2>Gates cause most escapes</h2>
<p>Most dogs that get out do it through a gate someone left open. Spring hinges that pull the gate shut, paired with a latch that catches on its own, fix that. Gate posts also take the most strain, so ours go deeper than line posts. That keeps the gate from sagging and leaving a gap at the latch side. See <a href="/fence-styles/gates/">gates and openers</a> for hardware options, and our <a href="/fence-styles/pet/">pet fence page</a> for materials. If you are weighing wood against vinyl for a dog yard, read <a href="/faq/wood-vs-vinyl-texas-climate/">wood vs. vinyl in our climate</a>.</p>
