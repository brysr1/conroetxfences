<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Around Conroe, the choice comes down to how much shade, sun, and falling debris your lot gets. Wood costs less and fixes board by board, but our wet, humid weather means it needs sealing every few years. Vinyl never needs stain and won't rot, yet it costs more and a heavy pine limb can crack a rail.</p>
</div>

<h2>What our rain and humidity do to each one</h2>
<p>Conroe averages roughly 51 inches of rain a year, and the air stays damp between storms. For wood, that means a steady cycle of soaking and drying. Boards swell, shrink, cup, and gray, and the bottom few inches stay wet longest. That is why every wood fence we build gets a rot board and why we push for a sealer in the first few months.</p>
<p>Vinyl ignores the water. It won't swell, rot, or feed termites. What humidity does to vinyl is cosmetic: a green film of mildew on whichever face gets the least sun. A rinse with mild soap takes it off.</p>
<p>The ground matters for both. Most lots here are sandy on top with slower-draining clay below, so water can sit around the base of a post after a big rain. Wood posts rot at that line. Vinyl posts don't rot, but they can lean if the hole wasn't set deep enough. We set every post in concrete either way.</p>

<h2>Wooded lots versus open, sunny lots</h2>
<p>The same material behaves differently on a shaded lot in Harper's Preserve than on a new, treeless lot in Mavera or Cielo.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Lots under pines and hardwoods.</strong> Shade keeps boards damp, and pine straw piles up against the bottom of the fence. Wood needs more attention here. Vinyl collects mildew faster but holds up structurally. The bigger risk is limbs: a cracked vinyl rail gets replaced as a part, while a broken wood picket is a few nails and a board.</li>
  <li><strong>Full-sun lots in newer subdivisions.</strong> Bare wood grays within a season or two on south and west runs. Vinyl does well in sun as long as it is a UV-stabilized product installed with room to expand.</li>
  <li><strong>Lake lots on the east side of Lake Conroe.</strong> Wind off the water and constant moisture favor vinyl or metal. Lots near the shoreline also have to stay off SJRA land below the 201-foot contour, whatever the material.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Cost, upkeep, and the HOA question</h2>
<p>On our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>, cedar runs about $30&ndash;$47 per installed foot, and vinyl privacy panels start around $50. Vinyl closes some of that gap over time because you skip the staining and the board replacements. If you plan to sell in a few years, wood usually makes more sense. Owners staying put for ten years or more who dislike weekend chores are the ones vinyl pays off for.</p>
<p>Before you pick, check the rules. Many Conroe-area associations write their fence guidelines around stained wood, and some allow vinyl only in certain spots. Checking the current guidelines for your address is part of every quote we write.
