<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Compare what you're actually getting, not just the total. Look at material quality and how the fence will be built: post depth and concrete, lumber grade and picket thickness, rails, fasteners, and gate hardware. Then check who handles 811 and approvals, and the warranty terms. If it isn't in writing, assume it isn't included.</p>

<h2>Ten things to get in writing</h2>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Item</th><th>What to look for on a Conroe-area job</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Materials</td><td>Species and grade for wood, picket thickness, metal gauge, and the hardware brand. Two quotes for the same 6-foot cedar fence can describe very different fences.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Post depth and concrete</td><td>A stated depth and concrete amount per post, with more at gates and corners. The common Conroe soil series is <a href="https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONROE.html" rel="noopener">loamy fine sand over slow-draining clay</a>, per the USDA, so a shallow post sits in loose sand and gives little grip.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Rails, rot board, fasteners</td><td>Rail count, whether a rot board is included, and what joins rails to posts. Nails alone are where cheap wood fences start to fail.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Texas 811</td><td>Who files the locate request for a new install, and whether you'll get the ticket number. Ask how they plan to avoid sprinkler lines and other private lines 811 doesn't mark.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Approvals</td><td>Which reviews apply to your address and who prepares them: a city permit, an HOA or POA committee, SJRA on Lake Conroe, or TRA on Lake Livingston.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Insurance</td><td>A certificate for general liability, and either workers' comp or a plain statement that the company is a non-subscriber.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Warranty</td><td>Length, start date, what it covers, and what it excludes. Storms and soil movement are usually excluded.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Payments</td><td>What's due at signing, what's due at the end, and what triggers the final payment. If you're financing, get the lender's terms in writing.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Tear-out and cleanup</td><td>Whether removing the old fence and its footings is on the quote, and what happens to dirt from the post holes.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Similar work</td><td>Photos or addresses of fences on lots like yours: wooded, sloped, lakefront, or acreage.</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h2>Questions that depend on where you live</h2>
<p>The same fence goes through a different process depending on which side of Conroe it's on. A good contractor will ask your address before talking price, and should be able to answer these:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Inside a city with its own rules?</strong> Willis lists a $50 contractor registration on its fee schedule, charges $75 for a wood or chain link fence permit, and <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/willis/latest/willis_tx/0-0-0-12804" rel="noopener">doubles the permit fee when work starts first</a>. Cleveland's <a href="https://tx-cleveland.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/3808" rel="noopener">fence permit application</a> asks whether the contractor's license or ID and liability insurance are on file with the city. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permit guide</a> covers Conroe, Panorama Village, and Huntsville.</li>
  <li><strong>A lot on Lake Conroe?</strong> Under SJRA's <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf" rel="noopener">Lake Conroe rules</a>, a permanent structure on Authority land below the 201-foot contour needs a permit. SJRA can order an unpermitted one removed at the owner's expense. Ask how the contractor will find that line before staking.</li>
  <li><strong>A powered gate?</strong> Inside Conroe city limits, the city's <a href="https://cms3.revize.com/revize/conroetx/Documents/Services/Building%20Inspections%20Permits/Permits/Electrical%20Permit%20Application%20(Fillable)_05.23.24.pdf" rel="noopener">electrical permit application</a> says electricians must be licensed with the state and registered with the city. Ask who is running the power.</li>
  <li><strong>Acreage?</strong> Ask whether they'll walk the whole line with you, and how they'll build corners and braces on a long wire run.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Reasons to pass on a bid</h2>
<ul>
  <li>A suggestion to build first and deal with the HOA or the city later</li>
  <li>A price given over the phone with no site visit for a lake lot or acreage</li>
  <li>Most of the money due before any material is delivered</li>
  <li>Vague answers about post depth, concrete, or how rails attach</li>
  <li>No way to reach the company after the job, which matters most after a storm</li>
</ul>
<p>Our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a> lists typical installed ranges, so a bid far below them is easy to spot.</p>
