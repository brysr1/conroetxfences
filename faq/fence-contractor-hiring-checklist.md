<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Compare what you're actually getting, not just the total. Look at material quality and how the fence will be built: post depth and concrete, lumber grade and picket thickness, rails, fasteners, and gate hardware. Then check who handles 811 and approvals, and the warranty terms. If it isn't in writing, assume it isn't included.</p>
</div>

<h2>The checklist</h2>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Get in writing</th><th>Why it matters around Conroe</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Material quality</td><td>Wood species and grade, picket thickness, metal gauge, and hardware. Two bids for a 6-foot cedar fence can use very different materials, and the cheaper one usually shows it within a few years.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Post depth and concrete</td><td>Most of the county has sandy soil over slower-draining clay, so shallow posts lean early. The quote should state the depth and how much concrete goes in each hole, with more for gate and corner posts.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Rails, bottom board, fasteners</td><td>How many rails, whether there's a rot board, and what holds rails to posts. These are where cheap wood fences fail first in our humidity.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Texas 811</td><td>The contractor should file the locate ticket and give you the number. Private lines like sprinklers aren't marked, so ask how they'll be avoided.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Which rules apply</td><td>City of Conroe, Willis, Panorama Village, or the county; HOA review; SJRA on Lake Conroe shoreline; TRA on Lake Livingston. Ask who checks and who files.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Insurance certificates</td><td>General liability and workers' comp, or a plain statement that the company is a workers' comp non-subscriber.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Warranty terms</td><td>How long it lasts, when it starts, and what's excluded. Storms and soil movement are commonly excluded, so read that part.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Payment schedule</td><td>How much is due at signing, when the balance is due, and what triggers it. If you're financing, get the lender's terms in writing too.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Tear-out, haul-off, cleanup</td><td>Whether the old fence and its concrete footings leave with the crew, and who deals with spoil from the post holes.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Photos of similar work</td><td>Lake lots, wooded lots, acreage, and HOA streets all build differently. Ask for examples that match yours.</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h2>Red flags worth walking away from</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Pressure to skip the HOA or the permit "to save time"</li>
  <li>No written quote, or no clear answer about what materials you're getting</li>
  <li>Cash only, or most of the money up front before materials arrive</li>
  <li>No answer when you ask about post depth or concrete</li>
  <li>No local phone number or address, especially right after a storm</li>
</ul>
