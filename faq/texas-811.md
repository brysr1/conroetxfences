<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Before anyone digs a post hole, the utilities with buried lines near your property get notified through Texas 811 and send locators to mark them. State law sets the timing: notice at least 48 hours ahead, not counting weekends and legal holidays, and no more than 14 days before digging starts. On our new installs the crew files that ticket. Your part is pointing out the private lines locators won't mark.</p>
</div>

<h2>What the law asks for</h2>
<p>The rules are in Chapter 251 of the Texas Utilities Code. The trigger is powered digging: <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.002" rel="noopener">section 251.002</a> defines excavation as using a motor, hydraulic tool, or other mechanized equipment to disturb soil 16 inches deep or more. A power auger setting fence posts meets that easily, and the <a href="https://texas811.org/homeowner/" rel="noopener">Texas811 homeowner page</a> lists fence projects among those that need a ticket.</p>
<p>Under <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.151" rel="noopener">section 251.151</a>, notice goes in at least 48 hours before digging, leaving out Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, and no earlier than 14 days ahead. A ticket filed on a Saturday clears at 11:59 a.m. the next Tuesday, or Wednesday if Monday is a holiday. When an HOA approval or a wet week pushes the start past that 14-day mark, the ticket gets refreshed.</p>
<p>That's a summary, not legal advice. The statute is on the Texas Legislature's site at the links above.</p>

<h2>How it fits into a Conroe-area job</h2>
<p>We file after the layout is staked, since the ticket describes the dig area. The contractor owns that ticket, so it's fair to ask anyone digging in your yard for the number. We'll give you ours.</p>
<p>Paint and flags show roughly where a line runs, not its exact spot. For pipelines, the Railroad Commission's rules define a <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/16-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-18-2" rel="noopener">tolerance zone</a> of half the pipe's diameter plus at least 18 inches on each side, and we treat every mark that way. If a planned post lands inside it, we respace posts instead of augering next to the line.</p>
<p>Water and sewer are the gap people don't expect. The statute sorts lines into two classes. Gas, electric, telecom, and cable are Class A, and <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/UT/htm/UT.251.htm#251.107" rel="noopener">section 251.107</a> requires those operators to join a notification center. Water and sewage lines are Class B and aren't covered by that requirement. Plenty of subdivisions around Conroe, Willis, and the east shore of the lake get water and sewer from a MUD or a small private utility. So a yard with no blue or green marks can still have pipe under it, and when something looks missing, we call the district.</p>

<h2>The lines nobody marks</h2>
<p>Locators mark the utility's side of the meter. Past it, the pipe and wire are yours. Around Conroe, that usually means:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Sprinkler mains and zone lines</strong> along the back fence in newer subdivisions like Grand Central Park, Evergreen, and Mavera, where they often run right along the rear fence</li>
  <li><strong>Low-voltage lighting and pool equipment wiring</strong> in finished backyards</li>
  <li><strong>Power and water to a shop, barn, or well pump</strong> on acreage around Willis, Huntsville, and Cleveland</li>
  <li><strong>Feeds to a boat lift or dock lights</strong> on Lake Conroe and Lake Livingston lots</li>
  <li><strong>Septic tanks and drain fields.</strong> Montgomery County's <a href="https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/document_center/1%20Environmental%20Health/How%20to%20Obtain%20a%20Septic.pdf" rel="noopener">septic permit guide</a> sets a minimum lot of 0.75 acre without a private well and 1.5 acres with one. On a lot that size, assume a tank and field lines somewhere, and maybe a well line too.</li>
</ul>
<p>Lake lots have one more wrinkle. Within 2,075 feet of Lake Conroe, <a href="https://www.sjra.net/lakeconroe/permitting-my-on-site-septic-facility/" rel="noopener">SJRA permits septic systems</a> instead of the county, so if you're near the water and don't know where your system runs, ask SJRA what it has on file for your lot. Elsewhere in the county, start with Montgomery County Environmental Health.</p>
<p>Tell us where you know lines run, and flag sprinkler heads before install day. If nobody knows where an old line goes, a private locating service can trace it for a fee. On acreage with decades of add-ons, that fee is well spent. Our <a href="/process/">process page</a> shows where locates fall in the schedule, and <a href="/resources/fence-posts-conroe-soil/">how we set posts in Conroe soil</a> explains why holes in loose sand sometimes run wider than you'd expect.</p>

<h2>If you're digging yourself</h2>
<p>Homeowners can file their own ticket by dialing 811 or through the <a href="https://texas811.org/homeowner/" rel="noopener">Texas811 homeowner portal</a>, and the request is free. Do it before setting a replacement post with a power auger, and wait out the full notice period. A ticket marks utilities, not property lines. If you're unsure where your line sits, read <a href="/faq/property-survey/">do you need a survey</a> before you mark anything.</p>
