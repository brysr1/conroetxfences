<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> A well-built wood fence here often lasts somewhere in the 10 to 18 year range, with cedar at the longer end and treated pine at the shorter end. Vinyl, aluminum, coated steel, and composite often go 20 years or more. On wooded, shaded, or wet lots, how the posts were set and how the fence is kept up can move those numbers a lot in either direction.</p>
</div>

<h2>Why the same fence ages differently across our area</h2>
<p>Conroe gets about <a href="https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/#dataset=normals-monthly&amp;timeframe=30&amp;station=USC00411956" rel="noopener">51 inches of rain a year</a> by NOAA's 1991&ndash;2020 normals, and the ground decides where that water sits.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Lots on wetter soils.</strong> Splendora fine sandy loam is one of the four most widespread soils in Montgomery County, and the USDA describes it as having a <a href="https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/S/SPLENDORA.html" rel="noopener">perched water table 15 to 28 inches down from December to April</a> in normal years. That is right where the bottom of a fence post sits, so a wood post there spends every winter wet.</li>
  <li><strong>Upland Conroe and Willis lots.</strong> The more common <a href="https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONROE.html" rel="noopener">Conroe series</a> has a sandy top over slow-draining clay. It dries faster at the surface, but loose sand lets posts work loose sooner. The same series was first described in Walker County, so Huntsville-area acreage behaves much the same.</li>
  <li><strong>Shaded, wooded lots.</strong> Under the pines in places like <a href="/neighborhoods/deer-trail-estates/">Deer Trail Estates</a> or lots backing to greenbelt, boards dry slowly after rain and pine straw banks against the base. Wood there ages faster on the shady side than the sunny one.</li>
  <li><strong>New-construction lots.</strong> In Grand Central Park, Artavia, and Mavera, fresh fill settles for a while after the house goes up, and gates may need adjusting early on. That isn't wear, but it shortens the life of a gate that's left dragging.</li>
  <li><strong>Cleveland and Lake Livingston.</strong> Texas A&amp;M lists <a href="https://urbanentomology.tamu.edu/urban-pests/termites/formosan/" rel="noopener">Formosan subterranean termites</a> in Liberty and Polk counties, so wood in soil contact is at more risk there. See <a href="/resources/termite-damage-wood-fence/">termite damage on wood fences</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>River and lake lows.</strong> Along the West Fork below the dam and on low lake lots, a fence can sit in saturated ground for days after a release. Our <a href="/faq/flood-zone-easement-fencing/">flood zone guide</a> covers designs that let water pass.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Habits that wear fences out early</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Sprinklers hitting the same pickets.</strong> Daily spray rots the bottom of a wood fence years ahead of the rest.</li>
  <li><strong>Unsealed wood.</strong> Bare boards gray, check, and split faster in our sun and humidity.</li>
  <li><strong>Limbs and vines.</strong> A fence under mature pines takes hits in every storm, and vines slowly pull mesh and boards out of line.</li>
  <li><strong>Light builder posts.</strong> Many newer subdivision fences went in on a tight budget, and some start leaning within a few years.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What adds years</h2>
<p>Most of the life of a wood fence is decided on install day. A 6-inch rot board keeps pickets off wet ground, posts sunk 24 inches or more and backed by 80 pounds of concrete hold their line, and Simpson brackets with hot-dipped galvanized ring-shank nails keep rails tight without rust streaks. Steel posts behind the pickets take ground-line rot out of the picture, which matters most on the wet lots above. See <a href="/materials/wood-cedar/">wood and cedar fences</a> for that option.</p>
<p>After that, a sealer in the first few months and a fresh coat every few years does the most good. Our <a href="/fence-staining-power-washing/">staining and power washing</a> crew handles that, and our <a href="/resources/fall-fence-maintenance-conroe-tx/">fall maintenance checklist</a> turns the rest into a yearly routine. Keep the base clear, aim sprinkler heads away, and trim limbs that hang over the line.</p>
