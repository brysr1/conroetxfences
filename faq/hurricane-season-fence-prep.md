  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. Before it starts, fix leaning posts and loose boards, check gate hardware, trim limbs hanging over the fence, and take dated photos of the whole line. After a storm, stay clear of downed lines, photograph the damage before cleanup, and get an itemized repair estimate.</p>
</div>

<h2>Why inland homes still prep</h2>
<p>Conroe sits well inland, but Gulf storms still reach it. The National Weather Service's <a href="https://www.weather.gov/hgx/hurricanes_climatology_2000s" rel="noopener">2000s hurricane summary</a> says Rita in 2005 brought gusts near hurricane force to Montgomery and Walker counties, with damage mainly to fences, roofs, and mobile homes. In Polk and San Jacinto counties, trees came down across power lines, and the Lake Livingston Dam recorded a 117 mph gust. Liberty County lost power for up to seven days. In 2008, the NWS <a href="https://www.weather.gov/hgx/projects_ike08_wind_analysis" rel="noopener">Ike wind analysis</a> found hurricane-force winds as far north as Livingston, and falling trees killed one person in Montgomery County and one in Walker County. Beryl's <a href="https://www.weather.gov/hgx/Beryl2024" rel="noopener">unofficial gust reports</a> in 2024 included 69 mph at Conroe.</p>
<p>The pattern is the same each time: wind on solid fences, and pines falling on everything.</p>

<h2>Before the season: a May walk-through</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Push each post.</strong> A post that rocks will be the first to go in a gust. Reset or replace it now, when the schedule is open.</li>
  <li><strong>Re-fasten loose pickets and rails.</strong> One loose board turns into a missing section in high wind.</li>
  <li><strong>Check gate hinges, latches, and drop rods.</strong> Gates take the most strain in a storm.</li>
  <li><strong>Look up.</strong> Dead pine limbs over the fence line are the most common cause of storm damage we see on wooded lots in Conroe and Willis. Have an arborist take down anything questionable.</li>
  <li><strong>Clear the base.</strong> Rake pine straw and brush away from the bottom of the fence so water can drain after heavy rain.</li>
  <li><strong>Photograph the fence.</strong> Date-stamped photos of every run show its condition before any storm, which helps if you file a claim later.</li>
</ul>
<p>Missed May? The National Hurricane Center puts the <a href="https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/" rel="noopener">peak of the season around September 10</a>, so a July or August walk-through still counts.</p>

<h2>When a storm is forecast</h2>
<p>Bring in anything that can fly into the fence: patio chairs, trampolines, trash cans, and loose lumber. Latch every gate and tie it shut if the latch is light. If you have an automatic gate, know where the manual release is and check its backup battery, since Beryl cut power to about 2.7 million people in Southeast Texas. On Lake Conroe and Lake Livingston, keep boat and dock gear away from fences near the shore, and close any side gate wide enough for a trailer.</p>

<h2>After the storm</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Stay safe first.</strong> A fence tangled with a downed power line stays off limits until the utility clears it.</li>
  <li><strong>Photograph before you clean up.</strong> Get wide shots of each damaged run and close-ups of broken posts, fallen trees, and debris. On low lots near the West Fork, photograph the debris line too, since wind and flood are separate claims.</li>
  <li><strong>Secure pets.</strong> A gap in the fence is an open door. A temporary panel or a leash run works until the repair is done.</li>
  <li><strong>Get an itemized estimate.</strong> We break out footage, posts, gates, and debris removal so you can see what was damaged and what the repair costs. Tear-out and haul-away show as their own line.</li>
  <li><strong>Call your insurer.</strong> The Texas Department of Insurance's <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/home-damaged-faq.html" rel="noopener">storm FAQ</a> says other structures, including fences, are usually covered up to about 10% of the home's insured amount, and fence coverage is usually limited to actual cash value, after depreciation and your deductible. Home policies don't cover flood. TDI also says temporary repairs are generally fine, but talk to your insurer before permanent ones. This is general information, not insurance advice, so check with your agent.</li>
</ol>
