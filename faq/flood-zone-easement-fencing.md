<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Usually, but both come with conditions. Inside Montgomery County's mapped floodplain, a fence may need a floodplain development permit, and inside a utility or drainage easement, the easement holder can often remove what's in the way at your expense. We check both before we lay out a single post.</p>
</div>

<h2>Two different things on the same lot</h2>
<p>A flood zone is a FEMA map designation for where water is expected to go in a major flood. It can cover the whole back half of a lot near a creek. An easement is a recorded right for someone else, such as a utility, a MUD, or a city, to use a strip of your land for lines or drainage. It shows up on your plat and survey. A lot can have one, both, or neither.</p>
<p>You can look up the current flood map for an address at the <a href="https://msc.fema.gov/" rel="noopener">FEMA Map Service Center</a>. For easements, check your <a href="/faq/property-survey/">survey</a> and the recorded plat. On a lake lot there is a third line to find, covered below.</p>

<h2>Who has a say, by location</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County.</strong> The county's <a href="https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/Development%20Regulations%20-%20Adopted%203-4-2025.pdf" rel="noopener">Development Regulations</a>, adopted in March 2025, list "installation of fences" in the definition of development and send any development in the floodplain to the Floodplain Administration for permitting. The county's <a href="https://www-04.landandfarm.com/api/documents/3983707421/Montgomery_County_Floodplain_Management_Regulations_.pdf" rel="noopener">floodplain management regulations</a> carve out one fence by name, three-strand barbed wire, so other fence types in the flood area may need a permit. For lots in the mapped floodplain, we confirm with the county floodplain office.</li>
  <li><strong>New subdivisions in the county.</strong> The same Development Regulations require fencing built along a new subdivision's edge to be built so it does not block drainage from offsite properties. That's a rule for developers, but it shows how the county looks at fences and water.</li>
  <li><strong>Walker County.</strong> Subdivision plats there carry a note requiring a development permit from the county floodplain administrator before man-made changes to the land. Whether that reaches a fence is a question for that office, and we ask it for Huntsville-area acreage.</li>
  <li><strong>Panorama Village.</strong> The city's fence packet doesn't allow fences in drainage easements at all.</li>
  <li><strong>City of Conroe.</strong> Inside city limits, start with the Building Inspections and Permits department. Our <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permit guide</a> covers each city.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Lake Conroe: 201 feet, then 207</h2>
<p>SJRA's <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Lake-Conroe-Rules-and-Regulations-2015.pdf" rel="noopener">Lake Conroe rules</a> treat land up to the 201-foot contour as Authority land, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit. Above that, SJRA's <a href="https://www.sjra.net/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/FAQs-Related-to-Harvey-and-Lake-Conroe-Dam.pdf" rel="noopener">Harvey FAQ</a> explains that a flowage easement recorded in the county deed records lets the lake rise to 207 feet, and operators work to keep it below that. On a sloped lot in Water Crest or a waterfront lot in Point Aquarius, the strip between those two lines is yours to fence, but it is built to take water.</p>

<h2>What the lake and the river have shown</h2>
<p>Lake Conroe is a reservoir with releases, and the water downstream rises with them. During Harvey in 2017, SJRA's peak release was about 79,000 cubic feet per second. In early May 2024, <a href="https://communityimpact.com/houston/conroe-montgomery/government/2024/05/03/flood-watch-dam-releases-continue-in-montgomery-county-as-waterways-crest" rel="noopener">releases reached about 70,000 cfs</a> with the lake peaking near 205 feet. That water runs down the West Fork past neighborhoods like <a href="/neighborhoods/river-plantation/">River Plantation</a>, where a 1994 flood led to the buyout of 20 homes and water came up again in 2015 and 2016.</p>
<p>On low lots there, and along the creeks that feed the lake, fences sit in saturated ground for days and catch whatever the water carries. A solid board fence turns that debris into a wall, and the wall pushes over. The Texas Department of Insurance notes that <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/consumer/storms/home-damaged-faq.html" rel="noopener">home policies don't cover floods</a>, and flood damage is outside our <a href="/warranty/">workmanship warranty</a>, so on a lot that floods, a design that sheds water is the practical choice.</p>

<h2>Fence designs that let water through</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Open <a href="/fence-styles/ornamental/">ornamental aluminum or steel</a>, which passes water and debris far better than solid boards. Both run about $40 to $100 a foot on our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>.</li>
  <li>Chain link, with the bottom held a few inches above grade where it crosses a swale</li>
  <li>An open bottom on the wood sections that cross drainage paths, instead of running the rot board straight through</li>
  <li>A gate or removable panels across an easement, so a utility crew can get in without cutting the fence</li>
  <li>Deeper, wider post holes in low, wet ground, which we size at the estimate</li>
</ul>
<p>None of this makes a fence flood-proof. It does make it more likely to be standing, and repairable, after the water goes down.</p>
