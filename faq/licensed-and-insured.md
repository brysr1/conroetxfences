<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Texas doesn't issue a state license to fence contractors, so "licensed" on a fence company's truck may not mean much. What's worth checking is insurance: current certificates for general liability and workers' compensation, plus a licensed electrician for any wired gate opener. 76 Fence of Conroe carries both policies, and we send certificates on request.</p>
</div>

<h2>Why there's no fence license to look up</h2>
<p>Texas once required home builders to register with the Texas Residential Construction Commission, but the state abolished that agency in 2009. No state agency licenses general contractors or fence builders today. So when a company says it's licensed, the useful follow-up is: licensed by whom, and for what?</p>
<p>Around Conroe, the honest answer is often a city. Some of the towns we work in keep their own records on contractors, and they differ:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Willis</strong> lists a $50 contractor registration on its <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/willis/latest/willis_tx/0-0-0-12804" rel="noopener">fee schedule</a>, alongside its fence permit fees.</li>
  <li><strong>Cleveland's</strong> <a href="https://tx-cleveland.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/3808" rel="noopener">fence permit application</a> asks whether a current license or ID and liability insurance are on file with the city.</li>
  <li><strong>Conroe's</strong> <a href="https://cms3.revize.com/revize/conroetx/Documents/Services/Building%20Inspections%20Permits/Permits/Electrical%20Permit%20Application%20(Fillable)_05.23.24.pdf" rel="noopener">electrical permit application</a> says electricians must be licensed with the state and registered with the City of Conroe before a permit is issued. That applies to the electrician, not the fence builder.</li>
</ul>
<p>Out in unincorporated Montgomery or Walker County, where a lot of acreage and some lake subdivisions sit, there may be no city in the picture at all. Then the homeowner is the only one checking who's doing the work.</p>

<h2>The two certificates to ask for</h2>
<h3>General liability</h3>
<p>This covers damage a contractor causes to your property or a neighbor's. On jobs around here that means things like an auger through a sprinkler main, a loaded truck cracking a driveway apron, or equipment ruts across a soft lawn after a week of rain. On a Lake Conroe lot it can mean a bulkhead or dock that sits close to the fence line. Ask for a certificate of insurance sent by the contractor's insurance agent, then check the company name, the policy dates, and the limits.</p>
<h3>Workers' compensation</h3>
<p>Texas lets most private employers opt out of workers' comp under <a href="https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LA/htm/LA.406.htm" rel="noopener">Labor Code section 406.002</a>. An employer without coverage is called a non-subscriber. Ask which one the company is, and whether the crew on your job are its employees or subcontractors with their own coverage. You can also check on your own: the Texas Department of Insurance <a href="https://www.tdi.texas.gov/wc/employer/coverage.html" rel="noopener">coverage verification page</a> links to a lookup for insured employers and to the state's list of registered non-subscribers. Your homeowners insurer can tell you how your own policy treats an injured worker.</p>

<h2>Gate openers and electrical work</h2>
<p>Electrical work is one trade Texas does license. TDLR's <a href="https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/elecfaq.htm" rel="noopener">electrical FAQ</a> says anyone performing electrical work in the state must be licensed, and that includes unincorporated areas. TDLR's <a href="https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/exemptions.htm" rel="noopener">exemptions page</a> carves out certain low-voltage, power-limited circuits. The 120- or 240-volt feed out to a gate is not one of them.</p>
<p>Solar openers skip the question, which is one reason they're common on long drives toward Huntsville and Lake Livingston. If you want a wired opener, ask who will run the power, and look up their license on TDLR's site. Our <a href="/resources/automatic-gate-opener-conroe-tx/">gate opener guide</a> compares solar and wired. We also build electric fence for livestock. If its charger needs a new circuit run out to it, that circuit is electrician's work too.</p>

<h2>Permits: whose name is on it</h2>
<p>If your city requires a fence permit, ask who applies and whose name goes on it. Skipping one puts the risk on the owner: Willis doubles the permit fee when work starts first, and Panorama Village inspects each finished fence. Where the city allows it, we're happy to apply for you. Some cities want the homeowner to submit, and then we help prepare the paperwork and drawings. If we pay the fee, it's passed through to you. The <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permit guide</a> covers each city near us.</p>
