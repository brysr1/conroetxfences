<div class="lc-callout">
  <p><strong>Short answer:</strong> Around Conroe, fences go in during every month of the year, so the best time is the one that fits your approvals and your plans for the yard. Spring and early summer book up fastest. If the fence has to be done by a set date, start the HOA and permit steps first, because they usually take longer than the build.</p>
</div>

<h2>Work back from the date you need</h2>
<p>The build is the short part. Most residential fences take a few days on site at most. What comes before it is what sets the calendar:</p>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Free estimate and written quote.</strong> We walk the line, measure, and send a quote with a drawing you can use for the HOA packet.</li>
  <li><strong>HOA or architectural review.</strong> Each committee meets when it meets, and a packet missing details gets sent back. Our <a href="/resources/hoa-fence-approval-conroe-tx/">HOA approval guide</a> covers what to send.</li>
  <li><strong>City permit, if one applies.</strong> In unincorporated Montgomery County, a fence outside the floodplain generally needs no county permit, but cities differ. In Panorama Village, the <a href="https://www.panoramavillagetx.gov/resident_services/planning_and_zoning.php" rel="noopener">Planning &amp; Zoning Commission</a> has up to 30 days to decide on an application. In Willis, the <a href="https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/willis/latest/willis_tx/0-0-0-12804" rel="noopener">city fee schedule</a> doubles the fee if work starts before the permit. Where the city allows it, we apply for you and pass the fee through; where the owner has to submit, we prepare the paperwork and drawings. See <a href="/faq/fence-permits-conroe-area/">permits by jurisdiction</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Texas 811.</strong> Before a new install, we request locates at least two business days ahead. See <a href="/faq/texas-811/">how 811 works</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Install.</strong> Weather and material delivery set the exact day.</li>
</ol>
<p>A new puppy, a pool fill date, or a listing photo shoot are all good reasons to book the estimate early. Count back from that date and leave the paperwork room to breathe.</p>

<h2>Season by season around Conroe</h2>
<p>The numbers below are NOAA's 1991&ndash;2020 normals for the <a href="https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/us-climate-normals/#dataset=normals-monthly&amp;timeframe=30&amp;station=USC00411956" rel="noopener">Conroe weather station</a>.</p>
<h3>Spring</h3>
<p>Our busiest stretch. New homeowners in Grand Central Park, Artavia, and Mavera want back yards closed in, older builder fences show their winter damage, and everyone wants the yard ready for summer. May is also the wettest month on average, at about 5.5 inches, so fronts can shuffle dates.</p>
<h3>Summer</h3>
<p>Hot, with average highs in Conroe of 94.9&deg;F in July and 95.8&deg;F in August. Crews start early, and concrete sets fine. July is one of the two driest months, but June runs over 5 inches. Hurricane season opens June 1, and a storm anywhere nearby fills the calendar with repair calls.</p>
<h3>Fall</h3>
<p>The National Hurricane Center puts the <a href="https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/" rel="noopener">season's peak around September 10</a>, and the season runs through November 30. Waits often ease as it winds down. October is nearly as wet as May, but the cooler days between rains make fall a good window for staining or sealing an existing fence, too.</p>
<h3>Winter</h3>
<p>A good time to book. The average January low in Conroe is about 42&deg;F, so hard freezes are the exception. Underbrush thins out on wooded acreage around Huntsville and Willis, which makes walking and clearing a new line easier. Low pastures and creek bottoms near Cleveland can stay too soft for equipment after a wet week, so we check the ground before we bring a machine in.</p>

<h2>Wet weeks and lake lots</h2>
<p>Rain doesn't cancel a job on its own, but saturated sand and water in the holes change how we set posts. The Conroe soil series is sandy on top with slow-draining clay below, per the <a href="https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/CONROE.html" rel="noopener">USDA description</a>, so a hole can fill overnight. In loose or wet ground we dig deeper or wider and clear the water before the pour, and on some lots it makes sense to wait a few days for the ground to drain. We'll call you if the weather moves your start.</p>
<p>Lakefront lots on Lake Conroe can stay soft for a while when the lake runs high after heavy rain, before SJRA releases bring it back down. For Lake Livingston and Huntsville, we line up nearby jobs on the same days, so flexible dates help there.</p>

<h2>Timing the stain on new wood</h2>
<p>New cedar or pine needs to dry out before stain will soak in. How long that takes depends on the lumber and the weather, so we check the boards rather than go by a fixed number of weeks. Building in spring and staining in fall is a common pairing here. When the wood is ready, we can handle that coat too; see <a href="/fence-staining-power-washing/">staining and power washing</a>. For budgeting both steps, start with our <a href="/pricing-guide/">pricing guide</a>.</p>
