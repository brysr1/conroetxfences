      <h2>Here since the lake was new</h2>
      <p>Seven Coves Association, Inc. was incorporated in 1972, the same era Lake Conroe itself was built and filled. The community sits on the lake west of I-45 in Willis. You get there by taking the FM 830 exit and heading west to the entrance at Kingston Cove.</p>
      <p>The association counts about 816 homesites. Building has gone on for decades, so a 1989 house may sit a few doors from one built in the 2000s, and fences vary just as much. Lots average about 0.22 acre, some on the water and some inside the neighborhood.</p>
      <p>Seven Coves is deed-restricted but not gated. A courtesy patrol covers the streets, which are curbed and drain to underground storm sewers. The association runs a marina with 288 slips, a private boat ramp, a fishing pier, a clubhouse, a pool, and boat and RV storage.</p>

Fence rules may differ from one section to the next. The online copies are scanned images, so we do not quote rules from them. We confirm your section&rsquo;s current requirements with the association before we quote.</p>

      <div class="lc-callout">
        <h3>Waterfront lots and SJRA land</h3>
        <p>The San Jacinto River Authority owns the land around Lake Conroe up to the 201-foot contour. A fence or other permanent structure on that strip needs an SJRA permit, so we locate your lot line before we plan the lake side. We usually suggest open, view-friendly fencing facing the water, a gate to the bulkhead or dock, and galvanized or stainless hardware that stands up to wind and wet weather.</p>
      </div>

If you keep a boat, kayaks, or fishing gear, that changes how a back yard fence should work. A wider gate, a latch you can open with one hand, and a clear path to the driveway matter more than they would in a dry subdivision.</p>
