      <h2>Before you fence in Point Aquarius</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="lc-prose">
      <h3>Architectural Control Committee</h3>
      <p>Any new fence or replacement goes to the POA&rsquo;s Architectural Control Committee. We did not find published fence guidelines, so we do not guess at heights, colors, or materials. We get the current requirements for your section and prepare a submittal with the layout, height, material, and finish. We build once you have approval.</p>
      <h3>If your lot touches the water</h3>
      <p>Around Lake Conroe, the San Jacinto River Authority owns the land up to the 201-foot elevation contour. A fence or other permanent structure on that strip needs an SJRA permit, and your survey line may stop short of the shoreline. We find that line before we plan anything near the water. On these lots we usually suggest:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Open, view-friendly fencing</strong> such as ornamental aluminum on the lake side, so the fence does not block the water.</li>
        <li><strong>A gate to the bulkhead or dock</strong>, placed where you actually walk down with gear.</li>
        <li><strong>Storm-ready hardware</strong>: galvanized or stainless fasteners, heavy hinges, and posts set deep enough for wind off open water.</li>
      </ul>
      <h3>Everything else</h3>
      <p>Point Aquarius has a Willis address, and a MUD serves it, but we have not confirmed whether it falls inside Willis city limits, so we check what applies to the address. Our crew also needs gate access for estimate and build days. And before any digging, we request Texas 811 locates.</p>
    </div>
    <p class="lc-note">POA requirements, SJRA rules, and permit requirements can change. We confirm the current rules for your lot before we quote.</p>
