      <h2>A river neighborhood with a long memory</h2>
      <p>River Plantation is one of the older communities south of Conroe. Building started in the late 1960s, and today there are about 1,200 homes spread along the banks of the San Jacinto River, roughly 6 miles south of town on the east side of I-45. You reach it on River Plantation Drive, which passes under the interstate.</p>
      <p>Most people who call us from here are not building a first fence. They are replacing one that has leaned, rotted at the posts, or taken a beating in high water. The river has flooded the neighborhood more than once: in 1994, 20 homes were bought out after a flood, there were floods again in 2015 and 2016, and Hurricane Harvey brought a mandatory evacuation in 2017.</p>
      <p>That history does not mean every lot floods. Plenty of homes sit higher. It does mean we ask about water on every estimate here, and we plan the fence around the answer.</p>

      <p class="lc-kicker">Plan first</p>
      <h2>Before you fence in River Plantation</h2>
      <p class="lc-lede">Lots here are older and not standardized, so we look at each one on its own terms.</p>
    </div>
    <ul class="lc-checks">
      <li><strong>Association review.</strong> The River Plantation Community Improvement Association (RPCIA) oversees the deed restrictions. We did not find a published fence guideline, so we confirm what it asks for before we quote.</li>
      <li><strong>Where water goes.</strong> We note low spots, drainage swales, and any path water has taken in past storms. A solid fence across that path can act like a dam and get pushed over.</li>
      <li><strong>Post setting for wet ground.</strong> Saturated soil loosens posts. On low lots we talk through galvanized steel posts, deeper holes, and concrete footings so the line stays plumb after a soaking.</li>
      <li><strong>Mature trees and roots.</strong> Many lots are shaded by big trees. We route the fence around trunks and large roots instead of cutting through them.</li>
      <li><strong>Old lot lines.</strong> Some owners have combined lots over the years; one listing joined three into about 0.72 acre. A current survey helps us set the line in the right place.</li>
      <li><strong>Outside city limits.</strong> River Plantation is in unincorporated Montgomery County, so City of Conroe permits do not normally apply. We confirm for your address.</li>
      <li><strong>811 before digging.</strong> Texas 811 marks gas, water, and cable lines for us ahead of the first post hole.</li>
    </ul>
    <p class="lc-note">Association requirements, county rules, and floodplain guidance can change. We check the current rules for your lot before we quote, and nothing gets built until approvals are in place.</p>
