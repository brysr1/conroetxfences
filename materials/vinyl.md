faqs:
  - q: "Does vinyl hold up in Texas heat?"
    a: "Good-quality vinyl is made with UV inhibitors to resist yellowing and chalky buildup. It does expand and contract with temperature, so we leave room for movement in the rails and panels instead of locking them tight."
  - q: "Will vinyl get moldy in a shady yard?"
    a: "It won't rot, but green mildew can form on the shaded side in humid weather. A garden hose and a mild soap solution take it off. Doing it once or twice a year keeps it looking clean."
  - q: "Can a damaged vinyl panel be repaired?"
    a: "Usually the damaged rail, picket, or panel is replaced rather than patched. Because vinyl comes in standard parts, swapping a section is quick as long as the color is still available."
  - q: "Is vinyl allowed in my neighborhood?"
    a: "It depends on the community. Some associations allow vinyl and others call for wood or metal in certain locations. We look up the current guidelines for your address before we quote."

      <h2>Clean lines that don&rsquo;t fade or rot</h2>
      <p>Vinyl fencing is made from PVC formed into posts, rails, and pickets that slot together. The color goes all the way through, so there is nothing to paint and nothing to peel. White is the classic finish, and tan, gray, and wood-grain textures are also made.</p>
      <p>Around Conroe it appeals most to people who are tired of resealing a wood fence every few summers, or who want a bright, even look along a front walk or a pasture entrance. It costs more than wood to install. Over the years, the money saved on stain and board replacement closes part of that gap.</p>
      <p>The catch is impact. A hard hit from a trailer or a falling limb can crack a rail, and that part gets replaced rather than patched.</p>

        <h3>Why people choose it</h3>
        <p><strong>Almost no upkeep.</strong> No stain, no paint, no sanding.</p>
        <p><strong>Moisture-proof.</strong> It won&rsquo;t rot, swell, or attract termites, which matters on damp Montgomery County lots.</p>
        <p><strong>Consistent look.</strong> Every panel matches, and the color stays put for years.</p>
        <p><strong>Several styles.</strong> Solid privacy, lattice tops, pickets, and two- or three-rail designs.</p>
      </div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>What to plan for</h3>
        <p><strong>Higher up-front cost.</strong> It sits above wood and below most ornamental metal and composite jobs.</p>
        <p><strong>Less forgiving of impact.</strong> Cracked parts are replaced instead of repaired.</p>
        <p><strong>Thermal movement.</strong> Rails grow and shrink with the temperature, so the install has to allow for it.</p>
        <p><strong>HOA limits.</strong> Not every community accepts it in every location.</p>
