faqs:
  - q: "Aluminum or steel: which should I pick?"
    a: "Aluminum can't rust, so it suits pools, lake lots, and damp shady yards. Powder-coated steel is heavier and stiffer, which helps on drive gates and security runs, but scratches need touch-up paint. We often mix them, with aluminum for the yard and steel for the gate."
  - q: "Is it really wrought iron?"
    a: "Almost never these days. The term is used for any black picket fence, but modern ornamental fencing is welded steel or aluminum with a powder-coated finish. It looks the same and costs less to maintain."
  - q: "Can you build near the water on Lake Conroe?"
    a: "Yes, with care. Land up to the 201-foot contour around Lake Conroe belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit. We confirm where your property line falls before setting posts near the shore."
  - q: "How do ornamental fences do in storms?"
    a: "Wind passes straight through the pickets, so they carry far less load than a solid fence. The usual storm damage is a limb landing on a panel, and a bent section can be replaced on its own."

      <h2>Open pickets, solid boundary</h2>
      <p>Ornamental fencing uses slim vertical pickets welded between two or three horizontal rails. People still call it wrought iron, but what goes in today is powder-coated aluminum or steel.</p>
      <p>Because you can see through it, it is the usual answer when the view is part of what you paid for: a cove on Lake Conroe, a golf hole, a pool, or a front garden. It still keeps kids and pets in, slows down anyone who doesn&rsquo;t belong, and gives the property a finished edge.</p>
      <p>Tops can be flat, spear-pointed, or finished with ball or quad finials. Black is the most common color, with bronze and other powder-coat colors available.</p>

      <p class="lc-kicker lc-kicker--light">Local conditions</p>
      <h2>Humidity, sun, and storm season</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="lc-grid lc-grid-4">
      <div><h3>Humidity &amp; rust</h3><p>Aluminum shrugs off damp air. On steel, we look for chips after install and recommend touch-up paint as soon as one shows.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Sun &amp; heat</h3><p>Factory powder coat stands up to strong sun far better than a brushed-on paint, without blistering.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Soil</h3><p>Posts go in concrete set for the lot, so panels stay level through wet and dry spells. Texas 811 locates come first.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Storms</h3><p>Open pickets let gusts pass. After a storm, check for limb damage and clear debris off the rails.</p></div>
