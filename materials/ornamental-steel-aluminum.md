faqs:
  - q: "Aluminum or steel: which should I pick?"
    a: "Aluminum can't rust, so it suits pools, lake lots, and damp shady yards. Powder-coated steel is heavier and stiffer, which helps on drive gates and security runs, but scratches need touch-up paint. We often mix them, with aluminum for the yard and steel for the gate. See [aluminum fences](/materials/aluminum/)."
  - q: "Is it really wrought iron?"
    a: "Almost never these days. The term is used for any black picket fence, but modern ornamental fencing is welded steel or aluminum with a powder-coated finish. It looks the same and costs less to maintain."
  - q: "Can you build near the water on Lake Conroe?"
    a: "Yes, with care. Land up to the 201-foot contour around Lake Conroe belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit. We confirm where your property line falls before setting posts near the shore."
  - q: "How do ornamental fences do in storms?"
    a: "Wind passes straight through the pickets, so they carry far less load than a solid fence. The usual storm damage is a limb landing on a panel, and a bent section can be replaced on its own."

      <p class="lc-kicker">The look</p>
      <h2>Open pickets, solid boundary</h2>
      <p>Ornamental fencing uses slim vertical pickets welded between two or three horizontal rails. People still call it wrought iron, but what goes in today is powder-coated steel or aluminum.</p>
      <p>Because you can see through it, it is the usual answer when the view is part of what you paid for: a cove on Lake Conroe, a golf hole, a pool, or a front garden. It still keeps kids and pets in, slows down anyone who doesn&rsquo;t belong, and gives the property a finished edge.</p>
      <p>Steel is the heavier of the two metals. Its pickets and rails resist bending, which is why it&rsquo;s our pick where a fence takes force. Tops can be flat, spear-pointed, or finished with ball or quad finials. Black is the most common color, with bronze and other powder-coat colors available.</p>

      <p class="lc-kicker">Good fit for</p>
      <h2>Where steel earns its price</h2>
    </div>
    <ul class="lc-checks">
      <li><strong>Drive gates.</strong> A wide gate hangs a lot of weight from one post. With an automatic opener, steel&rsquo;s stiffness keeps the frame true.</li>
      <li><strong>Street frontage.</strong> Where a car or mower could clip the fence, steel bends less.</li>
      <li><strong>Security runs.</strong> Heavier pickets slow down anyone who wants in.</li>
      <li><strong>Wooded lots.</strong> A heavy pine limb can bend a lighter panel that a steel one might survive.</li>
      <li><strong>Big dogs.</strong> A dog that hits the fence at a run won&rsquo;t flex a steel panel.</li>
      <li><strong>Mixed runs.</strong> Many lots use aluminum around the yard and steel for the drive gate.</li>
    </ul>

</section>

    <div class="lc-grid lc-grid-4">
      <div><h3>Humidity &amp; rust</h3><p>Steel depends on its coating. We look for chips after install and recommend touch-up paint as soon as one shows.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Sun &amp; heat</h3><p>Factory powder coat stands up to strong sun far better than a brushed-on paint, without blistering.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Soil</h3><p>Posts go in concrete set for the lot, so panels stay level through wet and dry spells. Texas 811 locates come first.</p></div>
      <div><h3>Storms</h3><p>Open pickets let gusts pass. After a storm, check for limb damage and clear debris off the rails.</p></div>

      <p class="lc-kicker">Rules &amp; permits</p>
      <h2>Local rules for steel fences</h2>
    </div>
    <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
      <li><strong>City of Conroe.</strong> Property inside city limits falls under the city&rsquo;s Building Inspections and Permits department, and Conroe&rsquo;s fence rules are in Chapter 14, Article XI of its code. Many Conroe-address subdivisions are in unincorporated Montgomery County instead.</li>
      <li><strong>Panorama Village.</strong> Golf-course lots need black aluminum or black wrought iron at 48 inches, with 3- to 4-inch openings and a smooth top rail. If you want steel, we confirm with the city that the panel qualifies before anything is ordered.</li>
      <li><strong>Lake Conroe.</strong> Land up to the 201-foot contour belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit.</li>
      <li><strong>HOAs.</strong> Lake and golf communities often ask for open, black metal fencing toward the water or course. We check your deed restrictions.</li>
    </ul>
