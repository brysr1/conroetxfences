faqs:
  - q: "Aluminum or steel: which should I pick?"
    a: "Aluminum can't rust, so it suits pools, lake lots, and damp shady yards. Powder-coated steel is heavier and stiffer, which helps on drive gates and security runs. We often mix them, with aluminum for the yard and steel for the gate. See [ornamental steel](/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/)."
  - q: "Will aluminum keep my dog in?"
    a: "For most dogs, yes. Small dogs can slip through standard picket spacing, so ask about tighter spacing or a mesh liner along the bottom. See [pet fencing](/fence-styles/pet/)."
  - q: "Does aluminum need painting?"
    a: "Not as routine upkeep. Factory powder coat holds up well in strong sun, and there's no steel underneath to rust if it gets scratched. A rinse now and then keeps it clean."
  - q: "Can you build near the water on Lake Conroe?"
    a: "Yes, with care. Land up to the 201-foot contour around Lake Conroe belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority, and permanent structures there need an SJRA permit. We confirm where your property line falls before setting posts near the shore."

      <h2>The wrought iron look, without the rust</h2>
      <p>From the street, an aluminum fence looks like black wrought iron: slim pickets welded between two or three rails, with a flat or spear top. Underneath the powder coat there&rsquo;s no iron, so a scratch from a trimmer or a dragged pool chair won&rsquo;t bleed orange down the pickets.</p>
      <p>It&rsquo;s lighter than steel, so panels are easy to handle and rack along a sloped yard. Around Conroe, where so many lots face a lake, a canal, a pond, a fairway, or a pool, it keeps the view open and still keeps kids and pets in.</p>
      <p>Black is the most common color, with bronze and other powder-coat colors available. Tops can be flat, spear-pointed, or finished with ball or quad finials.</p>
