  - q: "What is composite fencing made of?"
    a: "A blend of wood fiber and plastic pressed into boards with a wood-grain surface. Trex is the name most people recognize, and several other manufacturers make similar systems."
  - q: "Does composite fade?"
    a: "Most boards lighten a little during the first months in full sun, then settle into their long-term color. Darker colors also get warm to the touch in midsummer."
  - q: "Why does composite cost more than wood?"
    a: "The boards themselves cost more, and they are heavier, so the posts and framing have to be stronger. What you get back is a fence that skips the staining and board replacement that wood needs."
  - q: "Will my HOA accept composite?"
    a: "Some guidelines don't mention it yet, and others treat it like wood or like vinyl. We check what your community's current guidelines say and include product details in the submittal."

      <p class="lc-kicker">What it is</p>
      <h2>Wood character, plastic durability</h2>
      <p>Composite boards blend recycled wood fiber with plastic, then get a textured face that reads as wood grain from a few feet away. Brand-name systems like Trex are the familiar ones. The colors run from warm browns to modern grays.</p>
      <p>Because the boards are solid and straight, composite works well for full-privacy fences and for the horizontal designs that are popular on newer homes. It is a premium material, priced near the top of the range alongside ornamental metal.</p>
      <p>It suits people who plan to stay put. Once it&rsquo;s up, you won&rsquo;t spend weekends staining it.</p>

    <h2>Humidity, heat, and storms</h2>
    <p><strong>Humidity.</strong> This is where composite earns its price around Conroe. Damp air and wet ground don&rsquo;t rot it, and mildew on a shaded side washes off with soap and water.</p>
    <p><strong>Sun.</strong> Expect a small color change early on. Lighter colors stay cooler; dark ones look rich but heat up.</p>
    <p><strong>Soil and posts.</strong> The boards are heavy, so we often use steel posts or an aluminum frame, set for the clay or sand on your lot after a Texas 811 locate.</p>
    <p><strong>Wind.</strong> A solid composite fence catches as much wind as a solid wood one. Post spacing and depth matter as much as the boards.</p>
    <p><strong>Cleaning.</strong> A hose or a soft brush once or twice a year. Skip high-pressure washing close to the surface.</p>
  </div>
</section>
