faqs:
  - q: "Do I need a survey before fencing my acreage?"
    a: "On rural land, we strongly recommend one, or at least found and flagged corner pins. Old fence lines don't always follow the deeded boundary; one Texas Grand Ranch listing warned buyers about exactly that. We build to the line you show us, so it pays to be sure of it."
  - q: "What fence works well for horses?"
    a: "Most owners choose no-climb horse wire with a top board or rail, or pipe fence. Both are easy for a horse to see and hard to get a hoof through. Barbed wire is a poor choice around horses. We can mix types, such as pipe near the barn and no-climb on the back pasture."
  - q: "Can you clear the fence line?"
    a: "We can handle light brush and small trees along the line as part of the job. Heavy timber or dense thicket may need a clearing crew first, and we'll tell you that during the walk so it isn't a surprise."
  - q: "Are there HOA rules on acreage?"
    a: "Sometimes. Deed-restricted acreage communities such as Red Oak Ranch and Texas Grand Ranch have property owners' associations, while other rural tracts have none. We check what applies to your property before we quote."
  - q: "How far out do you travel?"
    a: "We work from our Conroe office north to Huntsville, east to Cleveland, and out to Lake Livingston, plus the acreage in between. See our [service areas](/service-areas/) for the full list."

      <h2>Long lines need strong corners</h2>
      <p>Fencing a few acres is a different trade from fencing a backyard. The runs go hundreds of feet, the ground rolls, pine and hardwood crowd the line, and a creek bottom usually sits right where you want the fence to go. The fence may also need to hold a horse, a few goats, or a dog that likes to roam, not just mark a boundary.</p>
      <p>We start at the corners. A well-built brace assembly is what keeps wire tight through wet springs and dry summers, so that is where the time and material go first. Then we choose the fence for each stretch: no-climb on the horse pasture, field wire for goats, board or pipe along the road, and gates wherever a truck, trailer, or tractor has to pass.</p>
