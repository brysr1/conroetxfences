faqs:
  - q: "How often should a wood fence be restained around Conroe?"
    a: "Every few years for most fences, but sun and shade make a big difference. A run that faces the afternoon sun or gets daily sprinkler spray fades and grays faster than a shaded one. When water stops beading on the boards, it is time."
  - q: "Semi-transparent or solid stain?"
    a: "Semi-transparent stain lets the grain show and wears gradually. Solid stain covers more like paint and hides mismatched or older boards, which helps after a repair. Many HOAs specify one or the other, so we check your guidelines first."
  - q: "Can you match my HOA's approved color?"
    a: "Yes. Tell us the color name, or let us look up your community's guidelines, and we'll stain to that color. If your fence was stained before, we'll make sure the new product works over the old one."
  - q: "My fence is gray and a few boards are loose. Stain or repair first?"
    a: "Repair first. Stain won't hold a leaning post or a rotted picket in place. We can fix the structure and refinish in the same visit. See [repair and maintenance](/maintenance-repair/)."

      <p class="lc-kicker">Around Conroe</p>
      <h2>Why wood grays so fast here</h2>
      <p>Conroe gets long, humid summers and heavy rain, often in the same week. Boards soak up water, dry out in the sun, then soak it up again. That cycle is what makes pickets cup, split, and turn gray, and the damp keeps mildew and green algae growing on shaded runs.</p>
      <p>Wooded lots see the most mildew. Shade and fallen pine needles hold moisture against the boards for days after a rain. Fences in full sun, like many on newer builder lots, have the opposite problem and fade faster.</p>
      <p>A good stain with UV protection and a water repellent slows both problems. It won&rsquo;t stop them forever, which is why we suggest planning a refresh every few years.</p>
    </div>
