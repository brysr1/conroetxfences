    <h2>Step 1 and 2: the estimate</h2>
    <p>When you reach out, the address tells us a lot. It shows whether the lot is inside Conroe city limits or out in the county, whether a property owners' association is involved, and whether you are near the lake. A rough length, a few yard photos, and the material you have in mind help us show up prepared. If it is a replacement or a repair, a photo of the existing fence helps too.</p>
    <p>Small repairs can sometimes be priced from photos. For a new fence, a strict HOA, a lakefront lot, or acreage, we come out and measure, because footage and grade change the price. On wooded lots we also look at which trees sit on the line, where roots will get in the way of post holes, and whether brush has to be cleared first.</p>
    <p>The quote comes in writing and covers the fence we talked through, including tear-out, haul-off, and any clearing. Our <a href="{{ '/pricing-guide/' | relative_url }}">pricing guide</a> lists typical ranges, and <a href="{{ '/financing/' | relative_url }}">financing through Hearth</a> is available if you would like to spread the cost out.</p>

<section class="lc-section lc-section--sand">
  <div class="lc-wrap">
    <div class="lc-head">
      <p class="lc-kicker">Step 3 in detail</p>
      <h2>Approvals, permits, and locates</h2>
      <p class="lc-lede">This step usually takes the most calendar time. It is also where the most expensive mistakes can happen, so we take care of it before any work starts.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="lc-grid lc-grid-2">
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>HOA and architectural review</h3>
        <p>Most of the master-planned and lake communities around Conroe have an HOA, a POA, or an architectural committee that reviews fences before they go up. Every community writes its own rules, and they change over time, so we confirm the current guidelines for your lot rather than assume. We then put together the submittal package: a site sketch showing the fence line, plus the height, material, style, and stain or color. Building starts after you have written approval.</p>
        <a class="lc-more" href="{{ '/hoa-fencing/' | relative_url }}">HOA fencing</a>
      </div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>City of Conroe permits</h3>
        <p>If your property is inside Conroe city limits, the City of Conroe Building Inspections and Permits department handles permits. Many subdivisions with a Conroe mailing address are outside the city, in unincorporated Montgomery County or the city's extraterritorial jurisdiction. Panorama Village is a separate city with its own rules. We find out which jurisdiction covers your address and what it requires, and we tell you before the quote is final.</p>
      </div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>Texas 811</h3>
        <p>Before we dig, we submit a Texas 811 request so utility companies can mark their buried gas, electric, water, and communication lines. Please wait for those marks before planting or trenching near the fence line. 811 does not mark private lines, like sprinkler pipe, landscape lighting, a line to a shop, or septic and well lines on acreage. Show us where yours run, and we will lay out the posts to avoid them.</p>
      </div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>SJRA on Lake Conroe lakefront lots</h3>
        <p>On Lake Conroe, the strip of shoreline up to the 201-foot contour belongs to the San Jacinto River Authority. Building a permanent structure on that land requires an SJRA permit. A fence on a lakefront lot can easily cross that line without anyone noticing, so we check the survey for where your deeded lot ends and SJRA land starts, and we plan the layout around it. On canal and bulkhead lots, we also plan the point where the fence meets the bulkhead.</p>
      </div></div>
    </div>
    <p class="lc-note">We never quote a permit fee, height limit, or HOA rule from memory. Each one depends on the address, and we confirm the rules for your property case by case.</p>
  </div>
</section>

    <div>
      <p class="lc-kicker">Step 4 in detail</p>
      <h2>Install day</h2>
      <p>The crew shows up with the materials listed on your quote. A typical residential fence takes one to three days on site. Longer runs, acreage, and custom gates take more time. Rain, late 811 marks, or a slow HOA can push the start date, and we will call you if that happens.</p>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
        <li><strong>Layout first.</strong> We stake the line and walk it with you before the first hole is dug.</li>
        <li><strong>Tear-out.</strong> If removing the old fence is part of the quote, it comes down and gets hauled away.</li>
        <li><strong>Posts.</strong> We set posts in concrete and size each hole for the soil we find. Gate posts and corners get extra depth.</li>
        <li><strong>Build and hang.</strong> We build the fence square and plumb and hang the gates so they swing freely.</li>
        <li><strong>Surprises get a conversation.</strong> If we hit buried concrete, a large root mass, or the footage changes, we stop and talk with you before any cost changes.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <h2>Step 5: walkthrough and aftercare</h2>
    <p>When the fence is finished, we walk it with you and compare it to what you approved. We open and close every gate and check each latch. Anything on the punch list gets fixed before we close out the job.</p>
    <p>Before we leave, we explain how to care for the material you chose. Wood does best with a sealer or stain once it has dried out, and sprinklers should be aimed away from the boards. Metal and vinyl mostly need a rinse now and then. As new posts settle through the first wet and dry seasons, a gate may stop latching cleanly. That is normal, and we will come back to adjust it at no charge.</p>
    <p>Our <a href="{{ '/warranty/' | relative_url }}">18-month workmanship warranty</a> starts on the completion date.
