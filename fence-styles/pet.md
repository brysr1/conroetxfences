faqs:
  - q: "How do I stop my dog from digging under the fence?"
    a: "The usual fixes are a bottom rail set below grade, a poured concrete strip along the base, or an L-shaped apron of wire along the base. We pick one based on how determined your digger is and what the fence is made of."
  - q: "What height works for a large dog?"
    a: "Six feet is the common answer for large, athletic dogs that can clear four feet. Smaller or calmer dogs do fine with four or five. Check what your HOA and lot allow before you decide."
  - q: "My dog barks at everything it sees. Will a solid fence help?"
    a: "Often, yes. A privacy or board-on-board fence removes the view of passing dogs and people, which calms many reactive dogs."
  - q: "Can you fence a dog area on acreage without fencing the whole property?"
    a: "Yes. A fenced yard off the back of the house, or a chain link or no-climb run, keeps the dog contained without the cost of fencing every acre."

      <h2>Start with the dog</h2>
      <p>There are three ways out of a yard for a dog: squeeze between boards, dig beneath the fence, or jump it. A good pet fence blocks whichever one your dog favors. A small terrier that squeezes through a three-inch gap needs very different spacing than a shepherd mix that jumps a four-foot fence without a running start.</p>
      <p>So the first questions at an estimate are about the dog: size, breed, age, and how it has gotten out before. Our GM, Byron Dean, often walks the yard with you and points out the weak spots, from a low corner to a gap under the gate.</p>

      <p class="lc-kicker">Local conditions</p>
      <h2>Heat, rain, and storms with a dog in the yard</h2>
      <ul class="lc-checks lc-checks--one">
        <li><strong>Heat.</strong> A dog outside in a Conroe summer needs shade and moving air. We talk about where shade falls and whether a solid fence should have some airflow near the ground.</li>
        <li><strong>Rain.</strong> Heavy rain settles soil and opens small gaps under an otherwise sound fence. Check the base after big storms.</li>
        <li><strong>Storm damage.</strong> One broken picket or a limb on the fence is an open door. Walk the line after a blow.</li>
        <li><strong>Gates.</strong> Self-closing, self-latching hardware keeps the yard closed when a guest forgets.</li>
        <li><strong>Digging.</strong> Utility lines are marked through Texas 811 before any post hole or dig barrier goes in.</li>
      </ul>
