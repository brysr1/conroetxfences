faqs:
  - q: "Why do gates sag?"
    a: "A gate hangs its whole weight on one post through two or three hinges. If the frame isn't braced, the hinges are too light, or the post moves in wet soil, the gate drops at the latch side. A rigid frame and a deeper gate post prevent most of it."
  - q: "Can you add an opener to the gate I already have?"
    a: "Often, if the gate and its posts are strong enough to carry the motor and the extra stress. We check both first and tell you if the gate should be rebuilt."
  - q: "Solar or wired opener?"
    a: "Solar works well where running power to the gate would be expensive, as on long acreage drives, as long as the panel gets sun. A wired opener makes sense when power is close and the gate gets heavy daily use."
  - q: "Does a new gate need HOA approval?"
    a: "It is usually reviewed with the fence, but some associations look separately at drive gates, keypads, or opener hardware. We include gate details in the submittal."
  - q: "My new gate needs a small adjustment. Is that normal?"
    a: "Yes. New posts settle a little in the first months. Call us and we'll come back and adjust it."

      <h2>The only part of the fence that moves</h2>
      <p>A gate gets opened, slammed, leaned on, and blown around every day. Instead of tying into a continuous line, it hangs from one post. That is why a weak frame or a post that has shifted in wet clay shows up at the gate long before the rest of the fence.</p>
      <p>We size each gate for what has to go through it, from a trash can or riding mower to a truck and trailer, then set the gate posts deeper and in more concrete than the line posts.</p>

      <h2>How each gate is built</h2>
    </div>
    <div class="lc-grid lc-grid-2">
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>Wood &amp; vinyl</h3>
        <p><strong>Wood</strong> gates get a diagonal brace or a welded steel frame inside so they stay square. <strong>Vinyl</strong> gates are built over a hidden metal frame, because vinyl on its own flexes too much to stay square.</p>
        <p><strong>Watch:</strong> wood gates need the same sealing as the fence; heavy wood drive gates need heavy hinges.</p>
      </div></div>
      <div class="lc-card lc-card--flat"><div class="lc-card-body">
        <h3>Steel &amp; aluminum</h3>
        <p><strong>Steel</strong> is the choice for driveways and security. <strong>Aluminum</strong> matches pool and yard fencing and won&rsquo;t rust.</p>
        <p><strong>Watch:</strong> steel needs touch-up paint at scratches; ornate custom gates are fabricated ahead and hung once the posts cure.</p>
      </div></div>

<section class="lc-section">
  <div class="lc-wrap">
    <div class="lc-head">
      <p class="lc-kicker">Local conditions</p>
      <h2>Keeping a gate swinging true</h2>
    </div>
    <ul class="lc-checks">
      <li><strong>Wind.</strong> A solid drive gate catches wind like a sail and puts that load on its hinges. We size hinges, posts, and bracing for wind as well as weight.</li>
      <li><strong>Wet clay.</strong> Gate posts are set deeper than line posts so they don&rsquo;t lean after a rainy season. Texas 811 locates come before digging.</li>
      <li><strong>Humidity.</strong> Hinges and latches rust or stiffen; a little lubricant twice a year helps.</li>
      <li><strong>Heat.</strong> Solar opener panels should face open sky, and batteries are replaced every few years.</li>
      <li><strong>After storms.</strong> Clear limbs from the gate path and check that the latch still catches.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
