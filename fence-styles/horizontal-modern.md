faqs:
  - q: "Do horizontal boards sag?"
    a: "Long boards can bow if the posts are too far apart. We space posts closer than on a vertical fence and often add a center support or top cap to keep the lines straight."
  - q: "Will my HOA approve a horizontal fence?"
    a: "Some associations accept it and some don't, especially where the fence faces a street. We check your community's current guidelines and include drawings in the submittal."
  - q: "Is a horizontal fence harder to climb?"
    a: "The boards can act like ladder rungs, especially with gaps. If that's a concern with kids or a pool, we can use tighter spacing or keep the horizontal design to areas where climbing doesn't matter."

      <p class="lc-kicker">The style</p>
      <h2>Boards turned on their side</h2>
      <p>A horizontal fence turns the boards on their side and stacks them between posts. Set tight, the boards give full privacy. With an even gap of a half-inch or so, the fence looks lighter and lets the wind through.</p>
      <p>It works with modern farmhouse and contemporary elevations, and around patios, pools, and outdoor kitchens where the fence is part of the view. Most are five to six feet tall.</p>
      <p>The building is more exacting than a standard picket fence. Posts go in closer together, every board has to line up, and the finish needs to protect the wood from our sun.</p>

      <p class="lc-kicker">Local conditions</p>
      <h2>Keeping a horizontal fence straight</h2>
    </div>
    <ul class="lc-checks">
      <li><strong>Humidity.</strong> Horizontal boards hold water on their top edges. A sealer and a top cap help them shed it.</li>
      <li><strong>Sun.</strong> A UV-protective finish keeps cedar from graying and checking.</li>
      <li><strong>Wind.</strong> Small gaps cut the wind load; solid designs need tighter post spacing.</li>
      <li><strong>Soil.</strong> Closer posts mean more holes, so we request Texas 811 locates and set every post for the lot.</li>
    </ul>
